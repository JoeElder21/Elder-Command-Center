# Preparation for Pre-Application Meetings with Agencies

## Purpose

Prepare a structured briefing package and talking-point agenda for a pre-application conference with a regulatory or planning agency in Kentucky. Pre-application meetings are voluntary but strategically important opportunities to identify potential objections, confirm submission requirements, and build a working relationship with agency staff before formal application.

## When to Use

- Before filing a zone map amendment, conditional use permit, or development plan with LFUCG Division of Planning or Louisville Metro Planning and Design Services.
- Before submitting a floodplain development permit application to the local floodplain administrator or KDOW.
- Before filing a KPDES permit application or Section 401 Water Quality Certification with KDEP Division of Water.
- Before submitting an encroachment permit or access point modification to a KYTC District Office.
- Before filing a subdivision plat with a county planning commission that operates under KRS Chapter 100.

## Required Inputs

1. **Agency and contact**: Name of the agency, the specific office or division, and the reviewing staff member if known.
2. **Project description**: Location (county, address, PVA parcel ID), acreage, current zoning, proposed use, and preliminary site concept.
3. **Application type**: The specific permit, approval, or entitlement being sought.
4. **Known issues**: Any site constraints, prior application history, or neighborhood concerns identified during due diligence.
5. **Project team**: Names and roles of attendees (owner, PE, PLA, attorney, developer).

## Procedure

### Step 1 -- Agency Research and Scheduling

- Identify the correct reviewing agency and division. Common Kentucky contacts include:
  - LFUCG Division of Planning (zoning, subdivision, development plans)
  - Louisville Metro Planning and Design Services (zoning, form district, subdivision)
  - Louisville MSD (stormwater, sanitary sewer capacity, floodplain in Jefferson County)
  - KDOW Floodplain Section (floodplain development permits outside Louisville MSD jurisdiction)
  - KDEP Division of Water, Surface Water Permits Branch (KPDES stormwater, Section 401)
  - KYTC District 5 (Fayette/central KY) or District 6 (Jefferson County) for state road access
  - USACE Louisville District, Regulatory Branch (Section 404 wetland/stream impacts)
  - Local fire marshal or fire protection district for access and hydrant review
- Request the meeting in writing (email) with a brief project description, proposed agenda, and list of attendees. Most agencies schedule within 2-4 weeks.
- Ask the agency to identify all staff who should attend (e.g., zoning reviewer, traffic engineer, stormwater reviewer, landscape plan reviewer).

### Step 2 -- Prepare the Briefing Package

Assemble a concise package (printed and digital) containing:

- **Project narrative**: One-page summary describing the site, proposed development, phasing, and key design features. State the specific approval being requested.
- **Vicinity map**: 1"=2000' or similar scale showing the site in context with surrounding land uses, major roads, and landmarks.
- **Aerial photograph**: Recent aerial with parcel boundary overlaid, annotated with adjacent zoning designations and land uses.
- **Preliminary site concept**: Sketch-level site plan showing proposed building footprints or lot layout, access points, open space, and stormwater management areas. Do not over-design at this stage; the goal is to communicate intent, not submit construction documents.
- **Constraint map**: Overlay showing floodplain boundaries (from FIRM panel), wetland areas (from NWI or field delineation), steep slopes exceeding 15% and 20%, karst features, easements, and setback lines.
- **Zoning summary sheet**: Current zone, proposed zone or use, relevant dimensional standards (setbacks, height, lot size, FAR), and how the concept meets or deviates from each standard.
- **Preliminary utility plan**: Diagram showing proposed connections to water, sewer, electric, and gas, with distances to nearest mains and estimated demand.
- **Traffic data**: Existing KYTC traffic counts on adjacent roads and a preliminary trip generation estimate using ITE Trip Generation Manual (current edition).

### Step 3 -- Develop the Question Agenda

Prepare a numbered list of specific questions organized by topic. Standard categories include:

**Zoning and Land Use**
- Does staff view the proposed use as consistent with the comprehensive plan's future land use designation for this area?
- Are there any pending comprehensive plan amendments or area studies that could affect this parcel?
- What conditional use or variance findings will be most scrutinized, and does staff foresee particular concerns with the KRS 100.213 findings of fact?
- Are there binding elements or development conditions from previous approvals on this site or adjacent parcels?

**Site Design**
- Does the proposed access location and configuration meet the agency's access management standards?
- Are there specific landscaping buffer or screening requirements along boundaries with residential zones?
- What open space or parkland dedication percentage applies (e.g., LFUCG typically requires 10% of gross area for subdivisions with 50+ lots)?
- Are tree preservation requirements triggered, and what survey methodology does the agency accept?

**Infrastructure and Utilities**
- Will the agency require a traffic impact study, and what scope (number of intersections, analysis scenarios) does staff anticipate?
- Is there sufficient sewer capacity for the proposed density, or will a capacity analysis be required?
- What stormwater detention and water quality standards apply, and does the agency have a preferred BMP type for this area?
- Are there any planned CIP projects (road widening, sewer upgrade, waterline extension) that could affect the development timeline?

**Process and Timeline**
- What is the current application review timeline from submission to hearing?
- Are there specific submission deadlines tied to planning commission meeting cycles?
- Will a neighborhood meeting be required or recommended before formal filing?
- Who are the key reviewing staff, and is there a single point of contact assigned to the project?

### Step 4 -- Conduct the Meeting

- Bring at least two copies of the briefing package: one for agency staff and one for your reference and note-taking.
- Begin with a brief project overview (5 minutes maximum), then transition to questions.
- Take detailed notes on every staff comment, recommendation, and concern. Record the name and title of each staff member who speaks.
- Ask staff to identify any issues they see as potential obstacles and what design modifications might address them.
- Request written follow-up from the agency confirming any substantive guidance provided during the meeting.
- Do not argue with staff positions; the purpose is information gathering, not advocacy. Save persuasion for the formal hearing.

### Step 5 -- Post-Meeting Documentation

- Within 48 hours, prepare a meeting summary memorandum documenting all agency comments, organized by topic.
- Distribute the memorandum to the full project team and the client.
- Update the project schedule and budget based on any newly identified requirements (e.g., additional studies, design modifications, extended review timelines).
- If the agency identified a concern that could be a project-stopper, flag it immediately to the client with a recommended path forward.

## Output Format

Produce two documents:

1. **Pre-Application Briefing Package**: Bound or tabbed set of exhibits as described in Step 2, formatted for 11x17 plan sheets and 8.5x11 narrative pages.
2. **Question Agenda**: Numbered list of questions organized by topic area, with space for recording agency responses during the meeting. Include a header block with project name, meeting date/time/location, attendees, and agency file number if assigned.

## Quality Checks

- The briefing package must be complete enough to communicate the project concept without verbal explanation, but not so detailed that it implies committed design decisions.
- All plan exhibits must include a north arrow, scale bar, legend, and date.
- Zoning references must cite the specific code section (e.g., LFUCG Zoning Ordinance Article 8-26 for the B-6P zone, not just "B-6P requirements").
- The question agenda must include process and timeline questions, not only technical questions; missing a submission deadline can delay a project by an entire review cycle.
- Traffic data must use current KYTC counts (available from the KYTC traffic count map online tool), not estimates.
- The constraint map must be based on actual data sources (surveyed boundaries, FIRM panels, NWI), not approximations from aerial photos.

## Template Reference

- ITE Trip Generation Manual (current edition) for trip generation estimates.
- KYTC Access Management Guide for state road access standards.
- LFUCG Zoning Ordinance and Land Subdivision Regulations for Lexington-area projects.
- Louisville Metro Land Development Code (LMCO Chapter 1) for Jefferson County projects.
- KDOW Floodplain Management Section application forms and guidance documents.
- KRS 100.211-100.214 for zone map amendment findings of fact and procedural requirements.

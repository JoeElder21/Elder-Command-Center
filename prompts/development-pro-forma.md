# Development Pro Forma Financial Analysis

## Purpose

Build a detailed development pro forma that models land acquisition, entitlement, design, construction, and disposition costs against projected revenues for a real estate development project in Kentucky. This tool enables a PE/PLA to advise clients on project feasibility, assist developers with infrastructure cost allocation, and support public incentive applications.

## When to Use

- Evaluating the financial viability of a proposed subdivision, commercial site, or mixed-use development.
- Preparing cost estimates to accompany a zone change application or development plan.
- Supporting a Tax Increment Financing (TIF) application under KRS 65.490-65.499.
- Analyzing infrastructure cost-sharing for a development agreement with a municipality.
- Advising a landowner on highest-and-best-use alternatives for a tract.

## Required Inputs

1. **Site data**: Acreage, PVA assessed value, proposed purchase price, topographic and geotechnical conditions.
2. **Program**: Number and type of units (lots, buildings, square footage), phasing plan, and absorption timeline.
3. **Jurisdiction**: Zoning jurisdiction and applicable impact fees, tap fees, and permit costs.
4. **Market data**: Comparable lot or unit sale prices, lease rates if applicable, and absorption rates from local MLS or CoStar data.
5. **Financing assumptions**: Loan-to-value ratio, construction loan interest rate, permanent debt terms, equity requirement.
6. **Infrastructure scope**: Roads, utilities, stormwater, grading, landscaping, and amenities to be constructed.

## Procedure

### Step 1 -- Land Basis and Acquisition Costs

- Record the contract purchase price or appraised value from the PVA assessment (note: Kentucky PVA assessments target 100% of fair market value per KRS 132.191, but actual market price may differ).
- Add closing costs: Kentucky deed transfer tax at $0.50 per $500 of consideration (KRS 142.050), title insurance, attorney fees, and survey costs.
- Include option or earnest money deposits and due diligence costs (Phase I ESA, geotechnical investigation, boundary survey).
- If assembling multiple parcels, model each acquisition separately and sum for total land basis.

### Step 2 -- Entitlement and Soft Costs

- Zone change application fees: LFUCG charges vary by application type; Louisville Metro planning fees are published in LMCO Chapter 1 fee schedule.
- Engineering and design fees: estimate at 8-12% of hard construction costs for civil engineering, landscape architecture, architecture, and surveying.
- Legal fees for zoning representation, deed preparation, and HOA document drafting.
- Environmental permits: KDOW floodplain development permit fees, USACE Section 404 permit fees (nationwide vs. individual), KY Phase II stormwater NOI under KPDES.
- KYTC encroachment permit and traffic study costs for access to state-maintained roads.
- Fiscal impact analysis or traffic impact study fees if required by the planning commission.
- Development plan and subdivision plat review fees per the local fee schedule.
- Builder's risk and general liability insurance during construction.

### Step 3 -- Hard Construction Costs (Site Infrastructure)

Develop line-item estimates for each infrastructure category:

- **Mass grading and earthwork**: Cut/fill quantities from preliminary grading plan; include unit costs for rock excavation if karst or limestone is anticipated (common in central Kentucky Bluegrass region).
- **Roads and paving**: Internal streets to local standards (Louisville Metro typically requires 26-ft pavement width in residential subdivisions; LFUCG standards are in the Land Subdivision Regulations). Include base, binder, and surface courses, curb and gutter, and sidewalks.
- **Sanitary sewer**: Gravity main installation per lineal foot, manholes, service laterals, and connection/tap fees. Louisville MSD tap fees are based on meter size; LFUCG charges capacity assurance fees.
- **Water distribution**: Main installation, fire hydrants at 500-ft spacing, service connections, and utility tap fees.
- **Stormwater management**: Detention/retention basins, water quality BMPs (bioretention, permeable pavers, level spreaders), storm sewer pipe, and inlet structures. Louisville MSD requires post-construction water quality treatment per their Design Manual.
- **Electric, gas, and telecom**: Coordinate with KU/LG&E, gas utility, and telecom providers; model developer-borne extension costs for underground utilities.
- **Landscaping and common areas**: Street trees per local requirements (LFUCG requires one street tree per 40 LF of frontage in many zones), entrance features, open space seeding and planting, and irrigation if applicable.
- **Erosion control**: SWPPP implementation costs for sites disturbing one or more acres (KPDES KYR10 general permit compliance).

### Step 4 -- Revenue Projections

- **Lot sales (subdivisions)**: Price per lot based on comparable sales; model absorption at realistic monthly rates (Kentucky residential subdivisions typically absorb 2-6 lots per month depending on market and location).
- **Vertical construction (if developer is building)**: Sale price per unit or per square foot, less construction cost of vertical improvements.
- **Commercial leasing**: Net operating income from projected lease rates and occupancy stabilization timeline.
- **Land residual (if selling entitled land)**: Premium for entitled, improved lots over raw land value.
- Apply an escalation factor for phased projects (2-3% annual price appreciation is a reasonable Kentucky baseline, though verify with local market data).

### Step 5 -- Financing and Carry Costs

- Model acquisition financing: loan amount, interest rate, and draw schedule.
- Construction loan interest calculated on average outstanding balance (use a monthly draw schedule aligned with the construction timeline).
- Loan origination fees, typically 0.5-1.0% of the loan amount.
- Real property taxes during development: calculate from PVA assessment; note that Kentucky reassesses upon subdivision platting, which can significantly increase the tax basis (KRS 132.450).
- HOA or maintenance costs during the sell-out period if developer is responsible for common area maintenance.

### Step 6 -- Incentive and Offset Analysis

- Model any applicable incentives: Kentucky TIF districts (KRS 65.490), local development incentive programs, Community Development Block Grant funds, or New Markets Tax Credits.
- Cost-sharing offsets: oversized utility reimbursements from the utility provider, KYTC cost participation for turn lane or signal improvements benefiting the state system.
- Development agreement credits for public infrastructure dedication (e.g., parkland dedication credit under LFUCG Subdivision Regulations).

### Step 7 -- Pro Forma Summary and Sensitivity Analysis

- Calculate total development cost, total projected revenue, gross margin, net profit, ROI, and IRR.
- Run sensitivity scenarios: 10% cost overrun, 20% slower absorption, 5% price reduction, and interest rate increase of 150 basis points.
- Identify the break-even absorption point (number of units/lots that must sell to cover all costs).

## Output Format

Present the pro forma as a structured financial summary:

1. **Sources and Uses table**: Total equity, debt, and other sources versus land, hard costs, soft costs, and contingency.
2. **Development budget**: Line-item detail for each cost category with unit costs, quantities, and totals.
3. **Revenue schedule**: Monthly or quarterly revenue projections by phase.
4. **Cash flow waterfall**: Period-by-period cash inflows and outflows showing cumulative position.
5. **Return metrics**: Total profit, profit margin, ROI, and IRR with sensitivity matrix.
6. **Risk summary**: Narrative identifying the top three financial risks and recommended mitigation strategies.

## Quality Checks

- Hard cost estimates must use unit prices appropriate to the Kentucky market and the current construction year, not national averages.
- All jurisdiction-specific fees must be sourced from the current published fee schedule (Louisville Metro, LFUCG, or applicable county).
- Revenue assumptions must cite comparable sales data with MLS numbers, addresses, or CoStar report references.
- Contingency must be included at a minimum of 10% of hard costs and 5% of soft costs.
- The financing model must reflect actual lender terms obtainable in the Kentucky market; contact local construction lenders for current rate verification.
- Sensitivity analysis must include at least three adverse scenarios and identify the break-even threshold for each.

## Template Reference

- Louisville MSD fee schedule and Design Manual for stormwater and sewer costs.
- LFUCG Land Subdivision Regulations for road and infrastructure standards.
- Kentucky Revenue Cabinet, Department of Property Valuation for PVA assessment methodology.
- KRS 142.050 (deed transfer tax), KRS 65.490-65.499 (TIF), KRS 132.191 (property valuation).
- RSMeans Site Work and Landscape Cost Data (annual edition) as a cross-check for unit pricing.

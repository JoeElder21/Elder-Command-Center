# Competitor Analysis for Engineering and Landscape Architecture Firms

## Purpose
Systematically evaluate competing firms or competing development projects to inform positioning, pricing, teaming, and pursuit strategy for a Kentucky-based civil engineering and landscape architecture practice. Produce intelligence that sharpens proposals, interviews, and strategic planning.

## When to Use
- Preparing for a shortlist interview or competitive proposal where the likely competitors are known or can be reasonably inferred.
- Evaluating whether to team with or compete against a specific firm on a public project.
- Annual strategic planning to reassess the firm's competitive position within Kentucky markets.
- A competitor announces a merger, acquisition, new office, or key hire that may shift market dynamics.
- Analyzing a competing land development project that affects a client's site or market.

## Required Inputs
- Names of competitor firms or competing developments to analyze.
- Target project or market segment for context (e.g., municipal water/wastewater in Eastern Kentucky, commercial site development in Central Kentucky, KYTC roadway design).
- Firm's own capability statement, project portfolio, and fee history for benchmarking.
- Publicly available data: KYTC prequalification lists, ACEC-KY membership directory, KSPE member directory, KALA membership, Kentucky Board of Licensure for Professional Engineers and Land Surveyors firm registrations, Secretary of State business filings.

## Procedure
1. **Identify the Competitor Set** -- List primary competitors (firms regularly competing for the same projects), secondary competitors (firms with overlapping capabilities but different geographic focus), and emerging competitors (new entrants or firms expanding into Kentucky). For development projects, identify the competing site, developer, and their design team.
2. **Firm Profile Assembly** -- For each competitor firm, compile: year founded, office locations in Kentucky, total staff count, number of licensed PEs and PLAs, KYTC prequalification categories, specialty certifications (CPESC, CFM, LEED, Envision SP), and leadership bios.
3. **Project History Review** -- Research the competitor's recent project awards using KYTC letting records, KIA project lists, public meeting minutes from fiscal courts and city councils, and local newspaper bid tabulations. Focus on projects in the target market segment within the past 3-5 years.
4. **Client Relationship Mapping** -- Identify the competitor's key client relationships by reviewing repeat project awards, public testimonials, advisory board memberships, and joint conference presentations at ACEC-KY, KSPE, KRWA (Kentucky Rural Water Association), or KLC (Kentucky League of Cities) events.
5. **Technical Capability Assessment** -- Evaluate the competitor's technical strengths and gaps. Review published case studies, award submissions (ACEC-KY Engineering Excellence Awards, KASLA design awards), and any publicly available project documentation. Note software platforms, modeling capabilities, and specialty services.
6. **Fee and Pricing Intelligence** -- Where available, review publicly disclosed fee proposals from government project awards (public record under KRS 61.870 Open Records Act for contracts with public agencies). Note fee structures: lump sum vs. hourly, overhead rates if disclosed, and any patterns in pricing relative to project size.
7. **Reputation and Differentiators** -- Assess the competitor's market reputation through industry contacts, client feedback (where ethically obtained), online presence, community involvement, and any regulatory actions or complaints filed with the Kentucky Board of Licensure.
8. **Strengths and Vulnerabilities** -- For each competitor, identify 3-5 strengths the firm must counter and 3-5 vulnerabilities the firm can exploit in positioning. Be specific: "Firm X has 4 PEs with KYTC bridge design prequalification" rather than "Firm X is strong in transportation."
9. **Positioning Strategy** -- Develop specific messaging and pursuit tactics that differentiate the firm from each identified competitor. Align differentiators to the evaluation criteria typically used by Kentucky public agencies (qualifications, experience, approach, capacity, local presence).

## Output Format
- Competitor Profile Sheets (1-2 pages each) summarizing key facts, strengths, and vulnerabilities.
- Comparison Matrix scoring the firm and each competitor on 8-10 evaluation criteria relevant to the target market.
- Positioning Statement drafted for use in proposals and interview presentations.
- Teaming Recommendation noting which competitors might be better as subconsultants or joint venture partners.
- Key Takeaways memo (1 page) with the 3-5 most actionable insights for leadership.

## Quality Checks
- All factual claims about competitors are sourced from public records, published materials, or direct observation -- no speculation presented as fact.
- Competitor staffing and licensure data verified against the Kentucky Board of Licensure online lookup.
- Fee data cited only from public records obtained through proper channels.
- Analysis avoids disparagement; focus is on objective differentiation, consistent with NSPE and ASLA codes of ethics.
- Comparison Matrix criteria are weighted to reflect actual evaluation criteria used by the target client or agency.

## Template Reference
- Competitor Profile Sheet template with standardized fields for consistent data collection.
- Comparison Matrix template (spreadsheet format) with weighted scoring columns.
- Positioning Statement template structured as: situation, differentiator, proof point, benefit to client.
- Teaming Decision Matrix template evaluating potential partners on complementary capabilities, geographic coverage, and past collaboration history.

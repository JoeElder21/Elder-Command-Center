# Post-Project Lessons Learned Documentation

## Purpose
Capture, document, and institutionalize knowledge gained from completed civil engineering and landscape architecture projects so the firm continuously improves its technical practice, project management, client service, and business outcomes. Lessons learned sessions convert individual project experience into organizational knowledge that reduces repeated mistakes and propagates successful innovations across Kentucky projects.

## When to Use
- At project closeout, after final deliverables are accepted and the project is financially closed.
- At the conclusion of a major project phase (e.g., after design is complete and before construction begins) for multi-year projects where waiting until the end would lose valuable context.
- After a significant project event: a claim, a design error, a construction change order exceeding 10% of construction cost, a permit denial, or an exceptional client compliment.
- Annually, as a firm-wide review of common themes across all projects closed during the year, feeding into the strategic planning process.

## Required Inputs
- Final project financial summary: original contract amount, approved amendments, total billed, total collected, write-offs, effective multiplier, and profit margin.
- Project schedule comparison: original baseline schedule vs. actual completion dates for key milestones.
- Client satisfaction data: formal survey responses, informal feedback, and any written commendations or complaints.
- Change order log and RFI log from construction administration (if applicable).
- QA/QC review records: plan review comments, errors found during checking, and any field-discovered design issues.
- Permit and regulatory submittal history: number of review cycles, comment themes, and approval timelines compared to assumptions.
- Subconsultant performance evaluations.
- Staff time allocation: budgeted hours vs. actual hours by task and by team member.

## Procedure
1. **Schedule the Session** -- Conduct the lessons learned meeting within 30 days of project financial closeout. Invite all team members who worked on the project, including the principal-in-charge, project manager, lead designer, CAD technician, field inspector, and any subconsultant leads. Allow 60-90 minutes for a standard project; 2-3 hours for large or complex projects.
2. **Pre-Meeting Data Package** -- Distribute a summary data package to all participants 3-5 days before the meeting. Include the financial summary, schedule comparison, client feedback, and top 5 change orders or RFIs by cost impact. Ask each participant to come prepared with at least 2 things that went well and 2 things they would change.
3. **Facilitate the Discussion** -- Structure the meeting around five categories:
   - **Scope and Fee**: Was the scope well-defined? Was the fee adequate? Where did scope creep occur? Were additional services captured and billed, or absorbed?
   - **Technical Execution**: What design approaches worked well? What would be done differently? Were Kentucky-specific standards (KDOW design criteria, KYTC design manuals, local ordinances) correctly applied from the start, or did review comments reveal gaps?
   - **Project Management**: Was the schedule realistic? Were milestones met? How effective was the communication plan? Were risks identified early and managed, or did they become surprises?
   - **Client Relationship**: How would you characterize the client's satisfaction? What drove satisfaction or dissatisfaction? Would the client hire us again? Did we earn a reference or a repeat engagement?
   - **Regulatory and Permitting**: How accurate were our assumptions about permit timelines and requirements? Did we encounter unexpected agency requirements? What would we do differently in pre-application coordination with KDOW, KYTC, or local planning staff?
4. **Identify Root Causes** -- For each negative lesson, push beyond the surface symptom to the root cause. Use the "5 Whys" technique. Example: "The KDOW permit took 120 days instead of 60" -- Why? "Because comments were extensive." Why? "Because the BMP sizing calculations used an outdated method." Why? "Because the firm's design guide hadn't been updated to reflect the 2023 KDOW BMP manual revision."
5. **Classify and Prioritize** -- Tag each lesson as: process improvement, technical knowledge, client management, business/financial, or regulatory/permitting. Rate the impact as high, medium, or low. Rate the likelihood of recurrence as high, medium, or low. Prioritize items that are high-impact and high-recurrence.
6. **Develop Action Items** -- For each prioritized lesson, define a specific corrective or improvement action:
   - Update a firm standard, checklist, or design guide.
   - Conduct a training session or lunch-and-learn for staff.
   - Modify the proposal or fee estimation process.
   - Adjust the QA/QC review checklist.
   - Update the regulatory checklist with new requirements or revised timelines.
   - Recognize and replicate a successful approach by adding it to the firm's best practices library.
7. **Document the Record** -- Prepare the formal lessons learned document. Each lesson entry includes: project name and number, category, description of the lesson, root cause, impact rating, action item, responsible party, and target completion date.
8. **Disseminate to the Firm** -- Present a summary of key lessons at the next all-hands or department meeting. Distribute the document to all project managers. Update the firm's lessons learned database or knowledge management system. For lessons with firm-wide implications, issue a technical bulletin or practice advisory.
9. **Track Implementation** -- Assign ownership of each action item to a specific person with a deadline. Review progress at quarterly management meetings. Close action items only when the improvement is verified as implemented (e.g., the design guide is updated, the training is delivered, the checklist is revised).

## Output Format
- Lessons Learned Report (3-5 pages) with:
  - Project summary: name, number, client, location, services provided, contract value, and duration.
  - Financial performance summary: fee, profit margin, and comparison to firm benchmarks.
  - Schedule performance summary: planned vs. actual for key milestones.
  - Lessons table: category, description, root cause, impact, recurrence likelihood, and action item.
  - Action item register with owner and deadline.
- One-page executive summary for leadership review.
- Updated entries in the firm's lessons learned database.

## Quality Checks
- Every team member who worked on the project was invited to participate; the session was not limited to management.
- Both positive and negative lessons are documented; the session is not solely a post-mortem for problems.
- Root cause analysis goes deeper than surface symptoms for every high-impact lesson.
- Action items are specific, assigned to a named individual, and have a deadline; no action reads "improve communication" without defining exactly what changes.
- Financial data is reconciled with the accounting system and signed off by the project manager.
- The document is filed in both the project record and the firm-wide lessons learned repository.
- Action items are tracked to completion at quarterly management reviews; the lessons learned process is not treated as a one-time event.
- Lessons involving regulatory agencies include the specific regulation, agency office, and reviewer name (if appropriate) to help future project teams navigate the same process.

## Template Reference
- Lessons Learned Meeting Agenda template with the five discussion categories pre-populated.
- Lessons Learned Report template with standardized table format for individual lesson entries.
- Project Financial Summary template pulling data from the accounting system for the pre-meeting data package.
- Action Item Register template with tracking columns for owner, deadline, status, and verification.
- Client Satisfaction Survey template (short form, 5-7 questions) to gather structured feedback before the session.

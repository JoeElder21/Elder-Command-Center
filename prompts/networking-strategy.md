# Strategic Networking and Relationship Building Plan

## Purpose
Develop a structured, intentional approach to professional networking and relationship building that generates project leads, strengthens the firm's reputation, and positions principals and staff as trusted advisors within Kentucky's civil engineering and landscape architecture community. Move beyond ad hoc conference attendance toward a measurable relationship development program.

## When to Use
- Annual business development planning to set networking priorities and budgets for the coming year.
- Onboarding a new business development lead or principal who needs to build a Kentucky-focused network.
- Entering a new geographic market or service area within Kentucky where the firm lacks established relationships.
- A key client contact retires or a major competitor poaches a relationship, requiring the firm to rebuild connections.
- Preparing for a specific pursuit where relationship building with the selection committee members is needed well in advance.

## Required Inputs
- Firm's target client list organized by sector: municipal, county, state agency, federal, utility district, private developer, institutional.
- Current relationship inventory: who in the firm knows whom, relationship depth (strong advocate, professional acquaintance, no contact).
- Calendar of Kentucky professional events for the planning period: ACEC-KY annual conference and legislative day, KSPE Engineers Day at the Capitol, KALA annual meeting, KRWA annual conference, KLC Annual Convention, KACo (Kentucky Association of Counties) annual conference, Area Development District quarterly meetings.
- Firm's budget allocation for memberships, sponsorships, event attendance, and hospitality.
- Staff availability and interest in networking activities; identify who is effective at relationship building.

## Procedure
1. **Relationship Audit** -- Catalog the firm's top 30 client relationships and top 20 referral relationships. For each, record the primary firm contact, last meaningful interaction date, relationship strength score (1-5), and any upcoming project opportunities. Identify gaps where the firm has no relationship with a key decision-maker.
2. **Target Identification** -- Using the market analysis and client onboarding data, identify 10-15 new relationship targets for the planning period. Prioritize by potential revenue, strategic importance, and accessibility. Include decision-makers at target organizations: mayors, city administrators, public works directors, utility board chairs, county judge-executives, planning commission chairs.
3. **Organization Engagement Plan** -- Select 4-6 professional organizations for active participation beyond passive membership. Prioritize organizations where target clients also participate:
   - ACEC-KY: committee service, PAC contributions, Young Professionals involvement.
   - KSPE: chapter officer roles, MATHCOUNTS mentoring, scholarship committee.
   - KALA/KASLA: design awards jury, continuing education presentations.
   - KRWA: exhibit booth, technical session presentations on water/wastewater design.
   - KLC: sponsor municipal training workshops, present at regional meetings.
   - KACo: attend county officials training, sponsor fiscal court workshops.
   - Area Development Districts: attend quarterly board meetings in target ADDs, offer technical assistance on grant applications.
4. **Content and Thought Leadership Calendar** -- Schedule 6-12 touchpoints per year combining: technical presentations at conferences, authored articles for Kentucky Engineer magazine or ACEC-KY newsletter, LinkedIn posts on Kentucky infrastructure topics, and lunch-and-learn sessions hosted for client staff.
5. **Event Attendance Matrix** -- Build a 12-month calendar assigning specific staff to specific events. Ensure coverage across geographic regions and client sectors. Set pre-event goals (e.g., "reconnect with 3 existing contacts and meet 2 new contacts at KRWA conference").
6. **One-on-One Relationship Development** -- For the top 10 priority relationships, schedule quarterly one-on-one interactions: site visits, project tours, meals, or office drop-ins. Track these in the CRM with notes on topics discussed, follow-up items, and relationship trajectory.
7. **Community and Civic Engagement** -- Identify 2-3 community boards, planning commissions, or civic organizations where firm staff can serve. Prioritize roles that provide visibility with target clients: local planning commission member, chamber of commerce board, Habitat for Humanity build captain, university advisory board.
8. **Measurement and Accountability** -- Define quarterly metrics: number of new contacts added to CRM, number of meaningful interactions logged, number of RFQ/RFP invitations received from networking efforts, and conversion rate from networking lead to signed contract.
9. **Budget Allocation** -- Allocate the networking budget across categories: memberships and dues, conference registration and travel, sponsorships, hospitality and meals, and promotional materials. Benchmark against ACEC fee survey data for firms of similar size.

## Output Format
- 12-Month Networking Strategy document with goals, assigned responsibilities, and calendar.
- Relationship Target List with current status, assigned firm contact, and next action for each target.
- Event Attendance Matrix (spreadsheet) mapping staff to events across the year.
- Quarterly progress dashboard tracking metrics against goals.
- Budget summary with line-item allocations and year-to-date actuals tracking.

## Quality Checks
- Every relationship target has an assigned firm contact and a specific next action with a deadline.
- Event attendance is distributed across staff, not concentrated on one or two people.
- The plan includes both "harvest" activities (pursuing known opportunities) and "seed" activities (building relationships with no immediate project in sight).
- Metrics are quantifiable and tracked at least quarterly.
- Budget is realistic relative to firm revenue and aligned with expected return on investment.
- Activities comply with KRS 11A (executive branch ethics) and KRS 45A (procurement) restrictions on gifts to public officials; no activity creates an appearance of impropriety.

## Template Reference
- Relationship Target Tracker template (spreadsheet with columns for contact name, organization, relationship score, assigned staff, last contact, next action, and notes).
- Event Attendance Matrix template (12-month calendar format with staff assignments).
- Networking ROI Calculator template linking networking investment to lead generation and revenue.
- Quarterly Networking Report template summarizing activities, metrics, and adjustments.
- CRM Contact Entry template with required fields for networking-sourced contacts.

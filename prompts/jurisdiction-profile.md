# Jurisdiction Profile: Development Requirements

## Purpose

Build a comprehensive reference profile of a Kentucky jurisdiction's development regulations, approval processes, fee structures, and staff contacts. This profile becomes the go-to reference document when initiating any project within that jurisdiction, eliminating repeated research and ensuring no regulatory requirement is missed.

## When to Use

- Entering a jurisdiction where the firm has not previously worked.
- Onboarding a new client with properties in an unfamiliar county or city.
- Updating an existing profile after a jurisdiction adopts new ordinances, fee schedules, or comprehensive plan amendments.
- Preparing a proposal that requires demonstrating familiarity with local regulations.

## Required Inputs

1. **Jurisdiction name**: City, county, or consolidated government (e.g., Louisville Metro/Jefferson County, LFUCG/Fayette County, City of Bowling Green/Warren County, City of Richmond/Madison County).
2. **Type of development**: Residential, commercial, industrial, mixed-use, or all types.
3. **Specific area of interest**: If the jurisdiction has multiple planning areas, overlay districts, or small area plans, identify the relevant geography.

## Procedure

### Step 1 -- Governance Structure and Authority

- Identify the governing structure: consolidated city-county (Louisville Metro, Lexington LFUCG), independent city with planning commission, county with planning commission under KRS Chapter 100, or county without planning and zoning.
- Determine who has final approval authority for each application type:
  - Zone map amendments: Planning commission recommendation to legislative body (fiscal court or city council) per KRS 100.211.
  - Conditional use permits: Board of adjustment per KRS 100.237.
  - Subdivision plats: Planning commission per KRS 100.277.
  - Development plans/site plans: Planning commission or staff-level approval depending on local delegation.
  - Variances: Board of adjustment per KRS 100.243.
- Record whether the jurisdiction has adopted its own subdivision regulations or relies on the minimum standards in KRS 100.273-100.291.
- Note whether the jurisdiction participates in the NFIP and identify the local floodplain administrator.

### Step 2 -- Zoning Ordinance Summary

- Obtain the current zoning ordinance and identify all zone classifications with their permitted uses, conditional uses, dimensional standards (setbacks, height, lot area, lot width, FAR), and parking requirements.
- Document form-based code or overlay districts if applicable (Louisville Metro uses form districts that operate alongside traditional zoning; LFUCG has adopted overlay zones for specific corridors).
- Record sign regulations, outdoor lighting standards, and landscaping/buffering requirements for each zone interface (e.g., commercial adjacent to residential).
- Identify any specific use standards for uses that frequently arise in practice: wireless telecommunications facilities (KRS 100.9865-100.987), solar energy systems, drive-through facilities, and home occupations.
- Note the comprehensive plan adoption date, the future land use map categories, and any pending amendments.

### Step 3 -- Subdivision Regulations

- Document the subdivision classification system: minor vs. major subdivision, administrative vs. planning commission approval thresholds.
- Record minimum design standards for:
  - Street width, pavement section, right-of-way width, cul-de-sac dimensions, and intersection spacing.
  - Minimum lot dimensions by zone.
  - Block length maximums and pedestrian connectivity requirements.
  - Sidewalk requirements (width, location relative to right-of-way, material).
  - Open space or parkland dedication formula (e.g., LFUCG requires land dedication or fee-in-lieu per the Parks Master Plan).
  - Street tree planting requirements (species, spacing, caliper).
- Identify required infrastructure improvements: whether the jurisdiction requires completion of improvements before plat recording or accepts a surety bond (KRS 100.277).
- Document the plat review and approval process, including submission deadlines relative to planning commission meeting dates.

### Step 4 -- Stormwater and Drainage Requirements

- Identify the stormwater management authority: Louisville MSD, LFUCG Division of Water Quality, or the local MS4 permit holder.
- Record the design storm standards for detention (typically 2-year, 10-year, 25-year, and 100-year events) and the required release rate methodology (pre-development vs. post-development comparison).
- Document post-construction water quality BMP requirements: which BMPs are approved, whether a BMP maintenance agreement is required, and who holds long-term maintenance responsibility.
- Note the erosion and sediment control permit threshold (typically one acre of disturbance, consistent with the KPDES KYR10 general permit).
- Record any stream buffer or setback requirements beyond the KDOW minimum (some jurisdictions impose wider riparian buffers than the state standard).
- Identify whether the jurisdiction has adopted a stormwater utility fee and whether credits are available for on-site management.

### Step 5 -- Utility Providers and Connection Requirements

Create a utility matrix:

| Utility | Provider | Contact/Phone | Tap/Connection Fee | Extension Policy | Design Standards Reference |
|---------|----------|---------------|-------------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Water | (e.g., KY American Water) | | Per meter size | Developer extends; utility reimburses oversizing | Utility design manual |
| Sanitary Sewer | (e.g., LFUCG Div. of Water Quality) | | Capacity assurance fee per unit | Developer builds to standards; dedicated to utility | Sewer design manual |
| Electric | (e.g., KU, LG&E, or RECC) | | Varies | Developer provides easements and conduit | Utility specifications |
| Natural Gas | (e.g., Columbia Gas, Atmos) | | Varies | Utility extends within service territory | Utility specifications |
| Telecom/Fiber | (e.g., AT&T, Spectrum) | | Varies | Coordinate with KYTC or local ROW permits | N/A |

- For each utility, document the process for obtaining a will-serve letter and the typical timeline.
- Note any special requirements: grease interceptors for food service, fire flow testing for commercial/multi-family, or backflow prevention device requirements.

### Step 6 -- Transportation and Access

- Identify all state-maintained roads (KYTC system) and locally maintained roads adjacent to or serving typical development areas.
- Record the KYTC district office contact and the encroachment permit process for access to state roads.
- Document the local jurisdiction's traffic impact study trigger thresholds (e.g., Louisville Metro requires a TIS when a development generates 100+ peak hour trips; LFUCG thresholds are in the Transportation Planning Section guidelines).
- Note any adopted access management regulations, required sight distance standards, and turn lane warrant criteria.
- Record the local complete streets or multimodal transportation policies, including bike lane, shared-use path, and transit stop requirements.

### Step 7 -- Fee Schedule

Compile the current fee schedule for all development-related applications:

- Zone map amendment, conditional use permit, variance, and development plan review fees.
- Subdivision plat review fees (preliminary and final).
- Grading permit fees.
- Building permit fees (typically per square foot or per valuation).
- Stormwater and erosion control permit fees.
- Utility tap and connection fees (water, sewer).
- Road and right-of-way encroachment permit fees.
- Impact fees if adopted (Kentucky authorizes impact fees under KRS 65.760-65.766 for some infrastructure categories).
- Park and recreation fees-in-lieu-of-dedication.
- Inspection fees for public infrastructure acceptance.

Record the source document and effective date for each fee. Note whether fees are updated annually or on a different cycle.

### Step 8 -- Key Contacts and Review Staff

Build a contact directory:

- Planning director and principal planners/reviewers.
- Zoning enforcement officer.
- Stormwater program manager.
- Public works director or county road engineer.
- Building official.
- Fire marshal or fire district chief.
- Floodplain administrator.
- KYTC district permit engineer.
- Utility company engineering contacts.
- Board of adjustment chair and planning commission chair (for hearing scheduling).

Include direct phone numbers, email addresses, and physical office addresses.

### Step 9 -- Approval Process Flowcharts

For each major approval type, document the step-by-step process:

1. Pre-application meeting (optional vs. required).
2. Application submission and completeness review period.
3. Staff technical review cycle (number of review rounds typical).
4. Public notification requirements: newspaper publication (per KRS 100.212), certified mail to adjacent property owners, posted sign on-site.
5. Planning commission or board of adjustment hearing date and format.
6. Legislative body action (for zone changes) and timeline.
7. Appeal period and process (KRS 100.347 circuit court appeal).

Note the typical total timeline from application filing to final approval for each application type.

## Output Format

Produce a single reference document organized with the following structure:

1. **Header**: Jurisdiction name, date prepared, date last updated, preparer name and contact.
2. **Table of Contents**: Hyperlinked to each section.
3. **Governance and Authority**: Narrative summary with organizational chart.
4. **Zoning Summary**: Table of zones with key dimensional standards; narrative on overlays and form districts.
5. **Subdivision Standards**: Table of design requirements by classification.
6. **Stormwater Requirements**: Standards table with design storm and BMP details.
7. **Utility Matrix**: Table as described in Step 5.
8. **Transportation**: Access standards, TIS thresholds, and multimodal requirements.
9. **Fee Schedule**: Comprehensive table with effective dates.
10. **Contact Directory**: Table with name, title, agency, phone, and email.
11. **Process Flowcharts**: Visual or tabular process maps for each major approval type.
12. **Appendix**: Links to downloadable ordinances, fee schedules, design manuals, and application forms.

## Quality Checks

- All ordinance references must cite specific chapter, article, and section numbers, not just the ordinance name.
- Fee amounts must include the source document name and effective date; mark any fee as "verify -- may have been updated" if the source is more than 12 months old.
- Contact information must be verified by phone or email within 30 days of profile creation; staff turnover is common in smaller jurisdictions.
- The profile must be reviewed and updated at minimum annually or whenever the jurisdiction adopts ordinance amendments.
- Stormwater design standards must be cross-checked against the jurisdiction's MS4 permit (KPDES) and any local design manual.
- The profile must distinguish between requirements that are codified in ordinance (mandatory) and staff preferences or informal policies (negotiable).
- For jurisdictions without planning and zoning, note that only state-level regulations (KDOW, KDEP, KYTC, building code under KRS 198B) apply, and clearly document the absence of local zoning controls.

## Template Reference

- KRS Chapter 100 (planning and zoning enabling statute) for procedural requirements.
- KRS 198B (Kentucky Building Code) for building permit authority.
- KRS 65.760-65.766 for impact fee authorization.
- LFUCG Zoning Ordinance (Articles 1-23), Land Subdivision Regulations, and Comprehensive Plan.
- Louisville Metro Land Development Code (LMCO Chapter 1), Cornerstone 2040 Comprehensive Plan.
- KYTC Access Management Guide and Highway Design Manual.
- KDOW Floodplain Management regulations (401 KAR 4:060) and NFIP community status listings.
- Kentucky Division of Building Codes Enforcement for statewide building code applicability.

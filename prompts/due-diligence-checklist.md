# Due Diligence Checklist for Land Acquisition

## Purpose

Generate a comprehensive due diligence investigation checklist for prospective land acquisitions in Kentucky, covering legal, environmental, geotechnical, infrastructure, and regulatory dimensions. This ensures a PE/PLA can advise a client on material risks before closing on a parcel.

## When to Use

- Client is evaluating a specific tract for purchase or option agreement.
- Preparing a feasibility study that will accompany an offer or letter of intent.
- Performing owner's due diligence before site plan or subdivision plat submittal.
- Reviewing a property for a public-private partnership or land swap.

## Required Inputs

1. **Parcel identification**: County, PVA parcel ID number (e.g., Fayette County PVA map/parcel), street address, and acreage.
2. **Proposed use**: Residential (single-family, multi-family, subdivision), commercial, industrial, mixed-use, agricultural, or conservation.
3. **County and jurisdiction**: Specify whether the site falls under Louisville Metro (LMCO), Lexington-Fayette Urban County Government (LFUCG), or a county with or without planning and zoning under KRS Chapter 100.
4. **Budget parameters**: Acquisition price and estimated development budget range.
5. **Timeline**: Target closing date and first phase of development.

## Procedure

### Phase 1 -- Title and Legal Review

- Pull the current deed and chain of title from the county clerk's office; verify legal description against PVA plat.
- Obtain a preliminary title commitment; flag easements, deed restrictions, liens, and right-of-way reservations.
- Check for active or expired mineral rights severances, which are common in eastern Kentucky coal counties (KRS 381.400 broad form deed issues).
- Confirm whether the property is subject to agricultural district enrollment under KRS 262.850, which imposes a 3-year notice period before conversion.
- Review any homeowner association covenants, conditions, and restrictions (CC&Rs) recorded against the parcel.
- Search for pending litigation or condemnation actions in the county circuit court.

### Phase 2 -- Environmental and Natural Resources

- Order a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment per ASTM E1527-21; if the site has historical industrial or agricultural chemical use, budget for Phase II sampling.
- Pull KDEP Underground Storage Tank (UST) database records and the EPA CERCLIS/Superfund list.
- Obtain the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) panel; determine whether any portion lies within Zone A, AE, or the 0.2% annual chance floodplain. For properties in NFIP communities, confirm the community's CRS class rating.
- Check the Kentucky Division of Water (KDOW) 303(d) impaired waters list for any streams on or adjacent to the site.
- Determine wetland presence using the USFWS National Wetlands Inventory and supplement with a field delineation if NWI polygons appear on-site. A USACE Section 404 permit will be required if wetlands are impacted.
- Review the Kentucky State Nature Preserves Commission database for threatened or endangered species occurrences within one mile.
- Evaluate karst potential: check the Kentucky Geological Survey sinkhole and karst inventory maps. Fayette, Jessamine, Scott, and Warren counties have significant karst terrain requiring geotechnical investigation.

### Phase 3 -- Zoning and Land Use Entitlements

- Confirm current zoning classification from the local planning commission (e.g., LFUCG zones R-1 through R-5, B-1 through B-6P, I-1/I-2; Louisville Metro form districts and zoning districts under LMCO Chapter 1).
- Determine if the proposed use is permitted as-of-right, requires a conditional use permit, or necessitates a zone map amendment under KRS 100.213.
- Review the adopted comprehensive plan and future land use map; evaluate consistency with proposed use per KRS 100.213(1) findings of fact.
- Identify overlay zones: historic preservation districts, airport noise contours (SDF or LEX), scenic corridors, or form district transition zones.
- Check subdivision regulation compliance: minimum lot size, frontage, block length, and open space set-aside requirements.

### Phase 4 -- Infrastructure and Utilities

- Contact the serving water district or utility (e.g., Louisville Water Company, Kentucky American Water) for available pressure and capacity at the nearest main. Request a will-serve letter.
- Contact the sanitary sewer provider (e.g., Louisville MSD, LFUCG Division of Water Quality) for capacity confirmation and whether a pump station or force main extension is needed.
- If no public sewer is available, evaluate on-site wastewater feasibility: Kentucky Division of Water requires soil evaluation per 902 KAR 10:085 for systems over 1,000 GPD; county health departments regulate smaller systems.
- Verify electric service availability (KU/LG&E, Kentucky Power, rural electric cooperatives) and gas service (Columbia Gas, Delta Natural Gas, Atmos Energy).
- Evaluate transportation access: check KYTC traffic counts on adjacent state routes, sight distance at proposed access points, and whether a traffic impact study will be triggered under KYTC access management standards.
- Confirm stormwater management obligations: Louisville MSD requires post-construction BMP plans for sites disturbing one acre or more; LFUCG follows the same threshold under their MS4 permit.

### Phase 5 -- Geotechnical and Topographic

- Commission a boundary and topographic survey per KRS 322.360 (licensed land surveyor requirement).
- Order a preliminary geotechnical investigation with borings at proposed building pad and stormwater facility locations.
- For sites with slopes exceeding 20%, evaluate the LFUCG or Louisville Metro hillside development regulations.
- In karst areas, conduct a sinkhole and subsurface void investigation using electrical resistivity imaging or dye-trace testing.

## Output Format

Produce a structured checklist document with the following for each item:

| Column | Content |
|--------|---------|
| Item # | Sequential number within each phase |
| Investigation Item | Description of the task |
| Source/Agency | Where to obtain the information |
| Status | Not Started / In Progress / Complete / N/A |
| Finding | Summary of result |
| Risk Flag | Green (no issue), Yellow (manageable), Red (material risk) |
| Cost Estimate | Estimated cost to complete the investigation item |

Include an executive summary section at the top that tallies risk flags and identifies deal-critical findings.

## Quality Checks

- Every item must identify a specific Kentucky agency, database, or statute rather than a generic reference.
- Environmental items must address both federal (NEPA, CWA Section 404, ESA) and state (KDEP, KDOW) regulatory layers.
- Infrastructure items must include the name of the actual utility provider serving that parcel, not a generic placeholder.
- Zoning analysis must reference the specific zoning code chapter and section applicable to the jurisdiction.
- All KRS citations must be verified as current (check the Kentucky Legislature LRC website).
- The checklist must be tailored to the proposed use; items irrelevant to the use should be marked N/A with a brief explanation, not deleted.

## Template Reference

- ASTM E1527-21 for Phase I ESA scope.
- FEMA Flood Map Service Center for FIRM panel retrieval.
- Kentucky PVA online portals for parcel data (qpublic.net or PVA-specific sites).
- Kentucky Geological Survey karst maps and county geologic quadrangles.
- KYTC Highway Plan and access permit application forms.
- KRS Chapter 100 (planning and zoning), KRS Chapter 224 (environmental protection), KRS Chapter 151 (water resources).

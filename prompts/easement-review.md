# Easement Review

## Purpose
Analyze easement documents and their physical location to determine how they constrain or enable development on a parcel. Identify conflicts between proposed improvements and existing easement rights, and recommend design adjustments or legal remedies.

## When to Use
- During due diligence before land acquisition when title work reveals easements
- At the start of site design when laying out buildings, utilities, and access
- When a utility company or adjacent owner asserts easement rights that conflict with a proposed plan
- Reviewing a proposed new easement for adequacy before recording

## Required Inputs
1. **Title commitment or title search** listing all easements of record with recording references
2. **Recorded easement documents** (plats, deeds, agreements) from the county clerk's office
3. **Boundary and topographic survey** showing easement locations plotted on the ground
4. **ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey** (if available) with Table A items for easements
5. **Proposed site plan** with building footprints, utilities, grading, and access

## Procedure
1. **Document Collection** -- Obtain all easement documents referenced in the title commitment from the county clerk's recording system. For each easement, extract: the grantee (who holds the rights), the purpose (utility, access, drainage, conservation), the width or area description, and any special conditions or termination provisions.
2. **Spatial Mapping** -- Plot each easement on the survey base map. For easements described by metes and bounds, verify the surveyor has plotted them accurately. For blanket easements or easements without a defined location, note the ambiguity and recommend a boundary line agreement or location survey.
3. **Conflict Identification** -- Overlay the proposed site plan on the easement map. Identify any proposed improvements that encroach into an easement: buildings within a utility easement, parking over a drainage easement, grading that alters an access easement, or landscaping that obstructs a sight triangle easement. For each conflict, note the specific easement provision that is violated.
4. **Rights Analysis** -- For each easement, determine: Can the grantee access the easement area for maintenance? Does the easement prohibit structures, grading, or paving? Is the easement exclusive or non-exclusive? Can the easement be relocated by agreement? Under Kentucky law (KRS 381.700-381.730), assess whether any prescriptive easement claims may exist.
5. **Utility Easement Review** -- Verify that proposed utility alignments have adequate easement coverage. Standard easement widths in Kentucky practice: 20-foot minimum for sanitary sewer and storm sewer (Louisville MSD typically requires 20 feet); 15-20 feet for water mains; 10-15 feet for gas and electric. Confirm that easements extend to a public right-of-way or connect to existing easement corridors.
6. **Recommendations** -- For each conflict, propose a resolution: relocate the improvement outside the easement, negotiate an easement relocation or abandonment with the grantee, obtain a variance from the local agency, or redesign utilities to avoid the conflict. Estimate the cost and timeline for each option.

## Output Format
- Easement summary table (easement name/number, recording reference, grantee, purpose, width, special conditions, conflict status)
- Annotated easement exhibit showing all easements on the survey with proposed improvements overlaid
- Conflict detail sheets (one per conflict with plan excerpt, easement language excerpt, and recommended resolution)
- Narrative memorandum (2-3 pages) summarizing findings and recommendations

## Quality Checks
- Every easement referenced in the title commitment has been reviewed in full, not just the schedule description
- Easement locations verified by a Kentucky-licensed Professional Land Surveyor
- Proposed utility easements meet the local utility provider's minimum width and access requirements
- Analysis distinguishes between appurtenant easements (run with the land) and easements in gross (personal to the grantee)
- Recommendations include estimated cost and timeline for resolution
- No proposed building footprint encroaches into any utility, drainage, or access easement without an identified resolution

## Template Reference
- KRS 381.700-381.730 (easement law)
- Louisville MSD Drainage and Sanitary Sewer easement requirements
- ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey Standards (Table A optional items)
- LFUCG Land Subdivision Regulations (easement width standards)

# Easement Review

## Purpose

Analyze easement documents -- including existing recorded easements, proposed easement language, and plat restrictions -- to determine their impact on site development, infrastructure placement, and long-term maintenance obligations for civil engineering and landscape architecture projects in Kentucky.

## When to Use

- During due diligence for a new development site before design begins
- When routing utilities through or adjacent to existing easement corridors
- Reviewing draft easement language prepared by the project attorney before execution
- Evaluating easement conflicts identified during title search or boundary survey
- When a public agency (Louisville MSD, KYTC, local utility) requests easement dedication
- Prior to subdivision plat recordation when new easements must be established
- When designing landscape improvements within or adjacent to utility easements

## Required Inputs

1. **Recorded easement documents** (deed book and page references from the county clerk's office)
2. **Title commitment or title search report** identifying all easements of record
3. **Boundary and topographic survey** (ALTA/NSPS survey preferred) showing easement locations
4. **Proposed site plan or construction drawings** showing planned improvements
5. **Utility atlas or GIS mapping** from serving utilities (Louisville MSD, LG&E/KU, Louisville Water, AT&T, Spectrum, local gas utility)
6. **Local subdivision regulations**: Louisville LDC Chapter 7, LFUCG Subdivision Regulations Article 16, or applicable county regulations
7. **Agency easement standards**: Louisville MSD easement requirements, KYTC right-of-way standards, local water utility easement widths

## Procedure

1. **Compile the easement inventory.** List every easement identified in the title commitment, recording the deed book/page or plat cabinet/page, grantee, easement type (utility, drainage, access, conservation), width, and any special conditions. Cross-reference with the surveyor's plat to confirm the easement location is accurately depicted.
2. **Classify each easement by type and impact.**
   - **Utility easements**: Identify the benefiting utility and confirm minimum widths meet their current standards (e.g., Louisville MSD requires 20 ft minimum for sewers up to 18-inch diameter, wider for larger mains; LG&E/KU typically requires 10-15 ft for underground electric and gas).
   - **Drainage easements**: Verify they encompass the full floodway or drainage swale, including maintenance access. Confirm widths meet KDOW or local stormwater manual requirements.
   - **Access easements**: Determine whether they are exclusive or non-exclusive, whether they permit vehicular access, and whether they have maintenance responsibility language.
   - **Conservation or environmental easements**: Check for restrictions on grading, tree removal, or impervious surface per KRS 382.800-382.860 (Kentucky Conservation Easement Act). Identify any deed restrictions related to the Kentucky Heritage Land Conservation Fund.
3. **Map easement conflicts.** Overlay all easements on the proposed site plan. Identify any proposed structures, retaining walls, fences, detention basins, or landscape plantings that encroach into an easement. Note that most utility easements prohibit permanent structures and trees with deep root systems within the easement corridor.
4. **Evaluate easement language for ambiguity.** Review each easement document for clear metes and bounds description or plat reference. Flag blanket easements (those without defined location) that could restrict development flexibility. Identify any easements with reversionary clauses, expiration dates, or conditions that could trigger termination.
5. **Assess prescriptive easement risk.** If the survey reveals improvements, pathways, or utilities not covered by a recorded easement, evaluate whether a prescriptive easement may have been established under Kentucky law (continuous, open, and adverse use for 15 years per KRS 413.010). Document and flag for attorney review.
6. **Review proposed new easements.** For easements being dedicated as part of the project, verify that the proposed width, alignment, and legal description match the site plan. Confirm language meets the requirements of the accepting agency. For Louisville MSD, verify the easement form matches MSD's standard easement template. For KYTC, verify the right-of-way description meets the KYTC Right of Way Division's requirements.
7. **Evaluate landscape design implications.** For PLA projects, identify plant species restrictions within easements (no trees within MSD sewer easements, no shrubs over 3 ft within LG&E sight triangles at intersections). Recommend plant material selections that comply with easement restrictions while meeting landscape code requirements.
8. **Document recommendations.** Prepare a summary of required easement actions: new easements to obtain, existing easements to relocate or vacate (requires the grantor's or successor's consent and a recorded release per KRS 382.070), and design modifications needed to avoid easement conflicts.

## Output Format

- Easement summary table: Easement ID, Type, Grantor/Grantee, Recording Reference (Deed Book/Page), Width, Length, Special Conditions, Conflict with Proposed Improvements (Yes/No), Recommended Action
- Easement exhibit map showing all easements overlaid on the proposed site plan (scaled, PE/PLA-sealed)
- Narrative memorandum summarizing critical findings, conflicts, and recommended resolutions
- Draft easement descriptions (metes and bounds by Kentucky PLS) for new easements to be recorded
- Redline markups of the site plan showing recommended design adjustments to resolve conflicts

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Every easement listed in the title commitment has been located on the survey and reviewed
- [ ] Blanket easements are flagged and their potential impact on development is assessed
- [ ] Proposed building setback lines respect all easement boundaries plus any required offset
- [ ] Utility easement widths meet the current standards of each serving utility
- [ ] No permanent structures are proposed within any utility or drainage easement
- [ ] Tree and landscape planting plans respect easement restrictions on root intrusion and access clearance
- [ ] New easement descriptions have been reviewed for closure and consistency with the survey
- [ ] Conservation easement restrictions under KRS 382.800-382.860 are fully identified and respected
- [ ] Attorney review is recommended for any ambiguous easement language or prescriptive easement risk
- [ ] All easement recording references (deed book/page, plat cabinet/page) have been verified at the county clerk's office

## Template Reference

- Louisville MSD Standard Easement Agreement form
- KYTC Right of Way Manual (Division of Right of Way and Utilities)
- LFUCG Subdivision Regulations Article 16 (easement requirements)
- Louisville LDC Chapter 7 (subdivision and easement standards)
- KRS 382.070 (release or amendment of easements)
- KRS 382.800 through 382.860 (Kentucky Conservation Easement Act)
- KRS 413.010 (prescriptive easement -- 15-year statute of limitations)
- ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey standards (current edition)

# Construction Cost Estimating

## Purpose

Develop reliable construction cost estimates for civil engineering and landscape architecture projects in Kentucky. Supports project budgeting, funding applications, bid evaluation, and value engineering decisions with estimates appropriate to the project phase -- from conceptual planning through detailed engineer's estimates at bid.

## When to Use

- Conceptual or planning-level estimates for capital improvement programs (AACE Class 5 or 4)
- Schematic design estimates for grant applications (CDBG, ARC, FEMA BRIC, KIA loans)
- Design development estimates for owner budget confirmation
- Detailed engineer's estimates at 90-100% construction documents (AACE Class 2 or 1)
- Value engineering analysis requiring cost comparison of design alternatives
- Opinion of probable construction cost for KYTC project development phases (planning, design, and PS&E)

## Required Inputs

- Project drawings and specifications at the current design phase
- Geotechnical report with soil classifications (USCS and AASHTO) and groundwater data
- Topographic survey and existing utility information
- KYTC weighted average unit bid prices (updated quarterly, published on KYTC website)
- RS Means Heavy Construction Cost Data or Site Work and Landscape Cost Data (current year, Louisville or Lexington city cost index)
- Kentucky prevailing wage rates by county (KRS 337.505 for public projects exceeding $250,000)
- Applicable sales tax rate (Kentucky 6% on materials)
- Known permitting fees (KPDES, floodplain, USACE Section 404, KY Division of Water)
- Project schedule assumptions for escalation calculations
- Local market conditions: recent bid results from similar Kentucky projects

## Procedure

1. **Quantity Takeoff**: Perform detailed quantity calculations from the design documents. Use digitized planimetric methods (Bluebeam, PlanSwift, or Agtek) for earthwork, paving, and grading. Calculate earthwork volumes using average end area or prismoidal methods; confirm cut/fill balance and identify haul distances. For linear utilities, tabulate pipe by material, diameter, and depth range. Count each structure, fitting, and appurtenance individually. For landscape items, quantify by species, size, and area (sod, seed, mulch in square yards or acres).

2. **Unit Price Development**: Establish unit prices from multiple sources, weighted by reliability. Primary sources for Kentucky: KYTC weighted average unit bid prices by district (most reliable for roadway, drainage, and bridge items), recent bid tabulations from comparable local projects (adjusted for scope and timing), and RS Means adjusted to Louisville (0.93) or Lexington (0.91) city cost index. For specialty items not in KYTC data (green infrastructure, decorative landscape features, custom structures), obtain budgetary quotes from suppliers and subcontractors.

3. **Crew and Production Rate Analysis (for AACE Class 2/1 estimates)**: Build unit costs from first principles when historical data is insufficient. Identify crew composition using RS Means crew codes or contractor input. Apply Kentucky prevailing wage rates (by county) for public projects. Calculate production rates accounting for soil conditions, weather limitations (Kentucky averages 120-140 productive field days per year for earthwork), and site access constraints. Include equipment costs from FHWA Equipment Watch or contractor rate schedules.

4. **Indirect Cost Calculation**: Add the following indirect costs as appropriate:
   - General conditions: typically 8-15% for civil/site projects (mobilization, bonds, insurance, supervision, temporary facilities, erosion control maintenance)
   - Performance and payment bonds: 1.5-2.5% of contract value (required on Kentucky public projects per KRS 45A.190)
   - Builder's risk and general liability insurance: 1.0-2.0%
   - Contractor's overhead and profit: 10-15% combined (KYTC assumes 10% OH + 10% profit for change order pricing)
   - Design and construction contingency: varies by phase -- 25-30% at conceptual, 15-20% at schematic, 10% at DD, 5% at CD/bid

5. **Escalation and Market Adjustment**: Apply escalation from the estimate base date to the anticipated midpoint of construction. Use ENR Construction Cost Index trends or KYTC historical bid price escalation data. In volatile markets, consider material-specific escalation for steel, PVC, concrete, and asphalt (Kentucky asphalt pricing is influenced by regional refinery output and KYTC letting volume). Apply a market conditions factor if bidding climate is unusually competitive or constrained.

6. **Estimate Organization**: Structure the estimate following CSI MasterFormat divisions or KYTC bid item numbering (for state highway projects). Group items into logical bid packages if the project may be phased or split. Provide subtotals by major work category (earthwork, utilities, paving, structures, landscape, erosion control). Clearly separate base bid items from alternates and allowances.

7. **Estimate Documentation**: Prepare a basis of estimate narrative describing assumptions, exclusions, sources of pricing data, site condition assumptions, and risk factors. Include a confidence range consistent with the AACE estimate classification. Identify the top five cost risks and their potential impact range.

## Output Format

- Detailed estimate spreadsheet organized by CSI division or KYTC bid item number with columns: Item Number, Description, Quantity, Unit, Unit Price, Extended Cost, Source/Basis
- Estimate summary page with subtotals by division, indirect costs, contingency, and total estimated construction cost
- Basis of estimate narrative (1-2 pages) covering assumptions, exclusions, pricing sources, and risk factors
- Unit price backup sheets for crew-based estimates showing labor, equipment, material, and production rate calculations

## Quality Checks

- Quantity takeoff has been independently spot-checked on the three largest cost items (typically earthwork, paving, and pipe)
- Unit prices are benchmarked against at least two independent sources (KYTC data, RS Means, recent bids)
- Earthwork balance accounts for shrinkage (typically 15-20% for Kentucky clay soils) and swell factors
- Contingency percentage is appropriate for the design phase per AACE recommended practice 18R-97
- Escalation rate and base date are clearly stated and reasonable (3-5% annually in stable markets)
- Sales tax is included on materials for projects where the contractor is not tax-exempt
- Prevailing wage rates are applied correctly by county for all public projects exceeding the KRS 337.505 threshold
- Estimate total is compared against parametric benchmarks (cost per linear foot of road, cost per acre of site development, cost per linear foot of sewer) from similar Kentucky projects

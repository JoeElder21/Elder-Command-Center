## 1. Executive Summary

This site analysis evaluates a 25.3-acre undeveloped parcel along Ring Road in Elizabethtown, Kentucky for a proposed mixed-use development consisting of retail, restaurant, and multi-family residential uses. The site is generally suitable for development with manageable constraints. Primary considerations include a Zone AE floodplain encroachment along the western boundary, Crider silt loam soils with moderate shrink-swell potential, and required coordination with the City of Elizabethtown Planning & Development Department for rezoning from A-1 Agricultural to B-3 Planned Commercial.

## 4. Soils and Geotechnical Conditions

### 4.1 USDA-NRCS Soil Survey Data

Soils data was obtained from the USDA Web Soil Survey for Hardin County, Kentucky (Survey Area KY093).

| Soil Type | Map Unit | Acreage | % of Site | Hydrologic Group |
|-----------|----------|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| Crider silt loam, 2-6% slopes | CrB2 | 14.8 ac | 58% | B |
| Crider silt loam, 6-12% slopes | CrC2 | 5.1 ac | 20% | B |
| Nolin silt loam, occasionally flooded | No | 3.6 ac | 14% | B |
| Elk silt loam, 2-6% slopes | EkB | 1.8 ac | 7% | B |

### 4.2 Soil Characteristics

**Crider silt loam (CrB2, CrC2)** -- 78% of site:
- Parent material: Loess over residuum weathered from limestone
- Depth to bedrock: >60 inches (typical 80-120 inches in this area)
- Depth to seasonal high water table: >72 inches
- Shrink-swell potential: Moderate (LEP 3-6)
- Bearing capacity (presumptive): 2,000-3,000 psf
- Percolation rate: 0.6-2.0 in/hr
- Suitability for shallow foundations: Good
- Frost depth: 24 inches (Kentucky Building Code)

**Nolin silt loam (No)** -- 14% of site (western corridor):
- Alluvial soils along Valley Creek floodplain
- Depth to seasonal high water table: 36-60 inches
- Flooding frequency: Occasional (5-50 year recurrence)
- Not suitable for building construction; appropriate for stormwater features
- Higher organic content; potential settlement concern if loaded

### 4.3 Geotechnical Recommendations

- A full geotechnical investigation (ASTM D1586 borings, minimum 8 locations) is recommended prior to design development. Boring depths should extend to 25 ft or refusal on competent limestone bedrock.
- Karst features (sinkholes, solution cavities) are common in Hardin County. A karst evaluation per KGS guidelines is recommended. No surface expressions of karst were observed during the site visit, but subsurface voids cannot be ruled out without borings.
- Proctor testing and California Bearing Ratio (CBR) testing should be included for pavement subgrade design.

## 5. Hydrology and Floodplain

### 5.1 Watershed and Drainage

The site lies within the Valley Creek watershed (HUC-12: 051401030603), which is a tributary to the North Fork of the Nolin River. Valley Creek flows north-to-south along the western boundary of the site before turning west approximately 0.2 miles south of the property.

| **Special Use Water** | No |

### 5.2 FEMA Floodplain

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **FIRM Panel** | 21093C0235E, effective 12/04/2019 |
| **Flood Zone** | Zone AE (western 3.4 acres along Valley Creek) |
| **BFE at Site** | 726.8 ft (NAVD88) |
| **Floodway** | Mapped floodway occupies western 1.2 acres |
| **Regulatory Freeboard** | 1.0 ft above BFE per City of Elizabethtown ordinance |

### 5.3 Floodplain Development Constraints

- No fill or structures are permitted within the mapped regulatory floodway (1.2 acres).
- Development within the Zone AE flood fringe (2.2 acres beyond the floodway) requires a Floodplain Development Permit from the City of Elizabethtown and a No-Rise Certification if within 500 ft of the floodway boundary.
- Finished floor elevations for any habitable structures must be a minimum of 1.0 ft above the BFE (minimum FFE = 727.8 ft).
- A CLOMR/LOMR may be required if the development proposes to modify the floodplain boundary through grading or fill placement.
- The 3.4 acres within Zone AE should be reserved for stormwater management, landscaping, and open space amenities (trails, passive recreation).

## 8. Environmental Considerations

### 8.1 Wetlands

A desktop National Wetlands Inventory (NWI) review indicates no mapped wetlands on the site. However, the Valley Creek corridor and Nolin silt loam soils along the western boundary exhibit hydric soil indicators. A formal wetland delineation per USACE 1987 Manual and Arid West Supplement is recommended prior to any grading within 200 ft of Valley Creek.

### 8.2 Threatened and Endangered Species

| Review Source | Finding |
|---------------|---------|
| **USFWS IPaC** | Indiana bat (Myotis sodalis) -- Endangered; potential summer habitat in mature trees along Valley Creek |
| **USFWS IPaC** | Northern long-eared bat (Myotis septentrionalis) -- Threatened; same habitat concern |
| **KSNPC Database** | No state-listed species records within 1-mile radius |
| **Recommendation** | Tree clearing should occur outside the active season (Oct 1 - Mar 31) or a presence/absence survey conducted |

### 8.3 Phase I ESA

A Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ASTM E1527-21) has not been completed. Given the agricultural history of the parcel, the following recognized environmental conditions (RECs) are anticipated based on historical aerial review:

- Former agricultural chemical storage area visible on 1988 and 1995 aerial photographs (SE corner)
- One former above-ground storage tank (AST) visible on 1995 aerials, not present on 2005 imagery
- No CERCLIS, RCRA, or UST database listings for the parcel (per EDR search)
- A Phase I ESA is recommended prior to acquisition or financing

### 8.4 Archaeological / Cultural Resources

The site has not been surveyed for archaeological resources. Given its proximity to Valley Creek and the general sensitivity of stream corridors in central Kentucky, a Phase I archaeological survey may be required by the Kentucky Heritage Council if any federal permits (USACE Section 404, FHWA) or funding are involved.

## 11. Preliminary Development Feasibility

The Heartland Crossing site is well-suited for mixed-use development. Key advantages include strong visibility and access from Ring Road, favorable topography across 74% of the site, available utilities with adequate capacity, and consistency with the adopted Comprehensive Plan. Primary challenges are the floodplain encroachment along the western boundary (which reduces developable area but provides an amenity corridor), the need for rezoning from A-1 to B-3, and the requirement for a Traffic Impact Study given the projected trip generation. These challenges are manageable and typical for a development of this scale in Elizabethtown.

### Recommended Next Steps

1. Pre-application meeting with City of Elizabethtown Planning & Development
2. Commission geotechnical investigation (8 borings, karst evaluation)
3. Commission Phase I ESA
4. Commission wetland delineation along Valley Creek corridor
5. Initiate Traffic Impact Study with KYTC District 4 coordination
6. Prepare rezoning application and development plan for B-3 Planned Commercial
7. Coordinate utility availability letters from E'town Water, E'town Sewer, Nolin RECC, and Atmos Energy

Brandon,

Thank you for reaching out, and I completely understand your concern about the schedule and budget on Briarwood Commons. You deserve a clear picture of where things stand and what we are doing about it, so I want to walk through the situation in detail.

## What Happened

During mass grading for Lots 12 through 28 on the east side of the site, the contractor encountered continuous limestone rock at an average depth of approximately 3.5 feet -- significantly shallower than what our geotechnical borings indicated (the borings showed rock at 7 to 9 feet in that area). As you may recall, the geotech report included four borings on the east parcel, but the subsurface conditions between boring locations turned out to be more variable than anticipated. This is an inherent risk with karst geology here in central Kentucky, and unfortunately it is one that materialized on this project.

The rock requires drilling and blasting to achieve the design grades for the building pads and utility trenches. We brought in Engleman Blasting out of Lexington, who has been working under an approved blast plan coordinated with the Hardin County fiscal court and adjacent property owners. That work is ongoing and approximately 60% complete.

In addition, the Kentucky Division of Water (KDOW) has required us to modify our KPDES stormwater permit (KYR10) due to the blasting operations and the increased volume of exposed rock on the grading surface. The modified permit adds additional water quality monitoring requirements at our two outfall points along the unnamed tributary to Valley Creek. We have submitted the Notice of Intent amendment, and KDOW's typical review period is 30 to 45 days, but we have been in direct contact with their Elizabethtown regional office to expedite the review.

## Cost Impact

Here is a summary of the additional costs we are tracking:

| Item | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|
| Rock drilling and blasting (Lots 12-28) | $78,500 |
| Additional haul-off of rock spoils | $12,200 |
| KPDES permit amendment and water quality monitoring | $4,800 |
| Blasting vibration monitoring (adjacent structures) | $3,500 |
| Revised grading plan engineering (Elder Engineering) | $6,200 |
| **Total Estimated Additional Cost** | **$105,200** |

I want to be transparent: the rock blasting figure could adjust modestly once Engleman finishes the east parcel, but I am confident that $78,500 is a reasonable estimate based on the quantities removed to date and what remains. I will firm up that number within the next two weeks as they complete the work.

## Schedule Impact

The original substantial completion date for site infrastructure was September 30, 2026. Based on the rock removal work and the permit modification timeline, we are now projecting substantial completion around November 7, 2026 -- approximately five and a half weeks of delay.

The breakdown:

- **Rock removal:** Added approximately 3 weeks to the grading schedule. The blasting contractor has been running a crew five days a week and we do not see a practical way to compress this further.
- **KPDES permit amendment:** Added approximately 2.5 weeks because certain grading activities near the outfall points must pause until the amended permit is in hand. We are working with KDOW to run this in parallel with the remaining rock work to minimize the overlap.

We do not anticipate further delays beyond this revised date, assuming normal weather through the fall. The western portion of the site (Lots 1-11) is proceeding on schedule and is unaffected by these issues.

I have confirmed with E'town Water and Hardin County Sewer (District No. 2) that the western utility connections will be ready for service by mid-August as originally planned.

4. **KDOW coordination.** I have a call scheduled with the KDOW Elizabethtown field office on July 18 to push for an expedited review of our permit amendment. I will update you on the outcome of that call.

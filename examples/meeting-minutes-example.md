## Project Overview (Presented by Joe Elder)

Joe Elder provided an overview of the proposed Hardin Crossing Mixed-Use Development on behalf of the applicant, Commonwealth Ventures, LLC. The presentation included a conceptual site plan exhibit (Exhibit A, attached to these minutes) and addressed the following:

- **Site:** Approximately 4.8-acre parcel on the east side of North Miles Street between College Street and Poplar Street. The site currently contains a vacant 22,000 SF former industrial building (Kentucky Barrel Company, vacated 2019) and associated paved lot. The applicant is under contract to purchase the property.

- **Proposed Program:**
  - Building A: 3-story mixed-use structure with ground-floor retail/restaurant (approximately 8,500 SF) and 24 residential apartments on floors 2 and 3.
  - Building B: 2-story commercial office building (approximately 12,000 SF).
  - Surface parking: approximately 145 spaces.
  - Shared courtyard and pedestrian plaza connecting Buildings A and B.

- **Zoning:** The parcel is currently zoned I-1 (Light Industrial). The applicant intends to request a zone change to B-3 (Central Business District) or alternatively seek a Planned Unit Development (PUD) overlay. The applicant is seeking the City's guidance on the preferred rezoning path.

## Discussion Items

### 1. Zoning and Land Use

**Amanda Phelps** stated that the proposed mixed-use program is consistent with the goals of the 2021 Elizabethtown Comprehensive Plan, which identifies the North Miles Street corridor as a target area for redevelopment with a mix of commercial and residential uses. She noted that a zone change from I-1 to B-3 could accommodate the proposed uses; however, the B-3 district does not currently permit residential density above 12 units per acre, and the proposed 24 units on 4.8 acres (approximately 5 units/acre net on the residential parcel area) would likely comply as long as the residential use is above the ground floor.

**Brian Koger** recommended the applicant consider the PUD overlay as an alternative to a straight B-3 rezone. He stated that the PUD process would provide more flexibility for mixed-use design standards (setbacks, parking ratios, building height) and would allow the Planning Commission to evaluate the project as a unified development with negotiated conditions rather than applying blanket B-3 requirements. He noted the PUD application fee is $750 plus $50 per acre ($990 total for this site).

**Kevin Harper** asked whether the PUD process would add significant time to the approvals. Phelps responded that the PUD follows the same general timeline as a zone change -- public hearing before Planning Commission with a recommendation to City Council -- and could be processed concurrently with the Development Plan application. She estimated approximately 8 to 10 weeks from a complete application submittal to City Council action, assuming no continuances.

**Action Item 1:** Elder Engineering to prepare a zoning comparison matrix (B-3 straight rezone vs. PUD overlay) evaluating setbacks, height, density, parking, and signage for the proposed development. Submit to Commonwealth Ventures and City Planning for review.
**Responsible:** Joe Elder, PE -- Elder Engineering
**Due Date:** July 31, 2026

### 2. Vehicular Access and Circulation

**Joe Elder** presented the proposed access plan showing two access points: a full-access driveway on North Miles Street (approximately 200 feet north of the College Street intersection) and a right-in/right-out driveway on College Street (approximately 150 feet east of the North Miles Street intersection).

**Amanda Phelps** stated that the North Miles Street access location appears acceptable based on the City's access management spacing standards (minimum 150-foot driveway separation). She noted that the City Engineer will need to review sight distance at both access points during Development Plan review.

**Brian Koger** asked whether a Traffic Impact Study (TIS) would be required. Phelps responded that based on the ITE Trip Generation Manual, the proposed development is likely to generate approximately 900--1,100 daily trips, which exceeds the City's 500-trip threshold for requiring a TIS. She confirmed that a TIS would be required and should be submitted with the Development Plan application.

**Lt. James Haycraft** stated that fire apparatus access must be maintained around the perimeter of Building A, which is 3 stories and therefore subject to the fire department access requirements in KBC 2018 Section 503. He requested that the site plan show a minimum 20-foot-wide fire lane with 26-foot inside turning radius at all corners, and that the fire lane be posted with no-parking signage per City ordinance. He also requested a fire hydrant within 300 feet of all portions of Building A's exterior, measured by hose-lay distance.

**Action Item 2:** Elder Engineering to coordinate with Client's traffic engineer (to be retained) to initiate the Traffic Impact Study. Scope and fee for TIS to be provided to Commonwealth Ventures within two weeks.
**Responsible:** Kevin Harper -- Commonwealth Ventures (retain traffic engineer); Joe Elder -- Elder Engineering (coordinate)
**Due Date:** August 7, 2026

**Action Item 3:** Elder Engineering to revise the conceptual site plan to show fire access lanes, turning radii, and preliminary fire hydrant locations per Lt. Haycraft's comments.

### 3. Stormwater Management

**Joe Elder** described the preliminary stormwater approach. The site is within the Valley Creek watershed. The existing site is approximately 75% impervious (building and asphalt). The proposed redevelopment will reduce overall impervious coverage to approximately 65% due to the addition of landscaped areas and the courtyard. Stormwater detention is anticipated to manage the post-development 2-year through 100-year storm events to pre-development release rates per the Elizabethtown Stormwater Ordinance.

**Amanda Phelps** noted that the City adopted updated stormwater management regulations in January 2025 that now require water quality treatment for the first 0.8 inches of rainfall (the "water quality volume") in addition to peak rate control. She recommended that Elder Engineering contact Jason Bland, PE, the City Stormwater Engineer, to discuss the specific water quality BMP requirements for this site. She noted that the City has been encouraging the use of bioretention and permeable pavement systems in the downtown area.

**Joe Elder** asked whether the City would accept an underground detention system given the site constraints. Phelps stated that underground detention has been approved on other B-3 sites with limited space and would likely be acceptable if properly designed with maintenance access and a recorded maintenance agreement.

**Action Item 4:** Elder Engineering to schedule a meeting with Jason Bland, PE (City Stormwater Engineer) to discuss water quality BMP requirements and underground detention feasibility for this site.
**Responsible:** Joe Elder, PE -- Elder Engineering
**Due Date:** August 1, 2026

### 4. Utility Connections

**Joe Elder** outlined the anticipated utility connections:

- **Water:** Connect to the existing 8-inch water main on North Miles Street (Hardin County Water District No. 1). Fire flow requirements for the 3-story mixed-use building (Building A) will need to be confirmed with the water district. Preliminary estimate is 2,000 GPM for 2 hours based on KBC 2018 and the ISO fire flow methodology.
- **Sanitary Sewer:** Connect to the existing 10-inch sewer main on College Street (Elizabethtown Sewer Department). Capacity confirmation will be required from the Sewer Department.
- **Electric:** Service from Elizabethtown Electric; transformer and service entrance locations to be coordinated with their engineering department.
- **Gas:** Service from Atmos Energy via existing main on North Miles Street.
- **Telecom:** AT&T and Spectrum service available in the corridor.

**Amanda Phelps** noted that the Elizabethtown Sewer Department has been requiring capacity studies for new connections in the College Street sewer shed due to known wet-weather capacity issues. She recommended contacting Darren Bales at the Sewer Department early in the process to determine if a Sewer System Evaluation Study (SSES) contribution or off-site sewer improvements would be required as a condition of connection.

**Action Item 5:** Elder Engineering to submit available-service request letters to all utility providers and request written confirmation of capacity and connection requirements.

### 5. Demolition and Environmental

**Kevin Harper** stated that the applicant has retained Terracon Consultants to perform a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) on the property given the former industrial use. The Phase I is underway and results are expected by the end of July 2026. If Recognized Environmental Conditions (RECs) are identified, a Phase II ESA with soil and groundwater sampling will be performed before demolition.

**Amanda Phelps** noted that a demolition permit from the City Building Inspector is required prior to demolition of the existing structure. She also noted that buildings constructed before 1980 may contain asbestos-containing materials (ACM) and lead-based paint (LBP), and that an asbestos inspection by a Kentucky-certified inspector is required before demolition per 401 KAR 63:002.

**Brian Koger** noted that the existing structure may be within or adjacent to the Elizabethtown Historic District and recommended that the applicant confirm with the Elizabethtown Historic Preservation Commission whether the building is a contributing structure. If so, demolition may require review by the Commission.

**Action Item 7:** Commonwealth Ventures to confirm with the Elizabethtown Historic Preservation Commission whether the former Kentucky Barrel Company building is a contributing structure to the historic district.

## General Notes

1. All storm drainage construction shall conform to KYTC Standard Specifications for Road and Bridge Construction, current edition, and applicable local jurisdiction standards.
2. All storm drainage pipe shall be installed to the lines, grades, and elevations shown on the approved construction drawings. Contractor shall verify invert elevations and pipe slopes before and after installation.
3. Minimum cover over storm drainage pipe: 12 inches from top of pipe to finished grade (24 inches under pavement areas or as required by pipe manufacturer for the applied loading).
4. All storm drainage systems shall be designed for gravity flow. No pumped stormwater systems without specific approval from the Engineer.
5. All pipe joints shall be watertight. Infiltration and exfiltration testing per ASTM C969 (RCP), ASTM F2487 (HDPE), or ASTM D3034 (PVC) as applicable.
6. Contractor shall dewater trenches as necessary to install pipe on dry, stable bedding. Do not place pipe in standing water.
7. Field verify all existing utility locations before trenching. Contact 811 (Kentucky 811) a minimum of 2 working days before excavation. Maintain minimum separation from existing utilities per Detail U-8 (Utility Separation Distances).
8. All drainage structures shall be precast concrete (ASTM C478) or cast-in-place concrete (4,000 psi minimum) unless otherwise specified.
9. Riprap at outfalls shall be KYTC Class II or as specified on the plans. Size riprap per expected velocity at design discharge.

## Detail D-1: Pipe Trench Cross-Section

### Description

Standard trench cross-section for storm drainage pipe installation. Trench width, bedding, and backfill requirements vary by pipe material and diameter.

### Dimensions & Specifications

**Trench Width**

| Pipe Diameter | Minimum Trench Width |
|--------------|---------------------|
| 12-24 inches | Pipe OD + 24 inches (12 inches each side) |
| 27-48 inches | Pipe OD + 30 inches (15 inches each side) |
| 54-72 inches | Pipe OD + 36 inches (18 inches each side) |

**Trench Section by Pipe Material**

| Layer | RCP (ASTM C76) | HDPE (ASTM F2306 / AASHTO M294) | PVC (ASTM D3034 / F679) |
|-------|----------------|--------------------------------|------------------------|
| Bedding | 4-6 in Class B granular (KYTC No. 57 or No. 8) | 4-6 in Class B granular (KYTC No. 57 or No. 8) | 4-6 in Class B granular (KYTC No. 57 or No. 8) |
| Haunch zone | Granular, hand-tamped under haunches | Granular, hand-tamped 70% up pipe diameter | Granular, hand-tamped 70% up pipe diameter |
| Initial backfill | Granular or select fill to 12 in above top of pipe | Granular to 12 in above top of pipe | Granular to 12 in above top of pipe |
| Final backfill | Native soil (no rocks > 6 in) in 8-in lifts | Native soil (no rocks > 6 in) in 8-in lifts | Native soil (no rocks > 3 in) in 8-in lifts |
| Compaction (initial backfill) | 90% Std. Proctor | 90% Std. Proctor | 90% Std. Proctor |
| Compaction (final backfill) | 95% Std. Proctor | 95% Std. Proctor | 95% Std. Proctor |
| Compaction (under pavement) | 98% Std. Proctor (top 12 in) | 98% Std. Proctor (top 12 in) | 98% Std. Proctor (top 12 in) |

### Notes

1. Do not compact directly over the pipe with heavy equipment until a minimum of 24 inches of cover is achieved above the top of the pipe.
2. For HDPE pipe: do not exceed 85% Std. Proctor compaction in the haunch and initial backfill zones — over-compaction can deform flexible pipe.
3. Trench bottom shall be uniform and free of rocks, debris, and point loads. Over-excavate soft spots and replace with compacted granular bedding.
4. Where groundwater is present, provide continuous granular bedding and consider geotextile wrap to prevent migration of fines.
5. Trench walls shall be braced, shored, or sloped in accordance with OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P for excavations deeper than 5 feet.

## Detail D-2: Pipe Bedding Classes

### Description

Bedding classifications per KYTC and AASHTO standards for rigid pipe (RCP) installations. Bedding class determines the load-carrying capacity of the pipe-soil system.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Bedding Class | Description | Bedding Material | Compaction | D-Load Factor |
|--------------|-------------|-----------------|------------|--------------|
| **Class A** | Concrete cradle or arch | Concrete encasement (full or partial) | N/A — poured concrete | 2.2 - 3.4 |
| **Class B** | Shaped granular bedding | KYTC No. 57 or No. 8 stone; shaped to fit lower 1/4 of pipe | Hand-tamped, 90% Std. Proctor | 1.9 |
| **Class C** | Granular bedding (standard) | KYTC No. 57 or No. 8 stone; flat bottom, 4-6 in thick | Hand-tamped, 90% Std. Proctor | 1.5 |
| **Class D** | Flat bottom, native soil | Native soil, no shaping | Minimal | 1.1 |

### Notes

1. Class B bedding is the standard for JEDS projects unless otherwise specified.
2. Class A (concrete encasement) required where: cover is less than minimum, pipe crosses under structures or retaining walls, or pipe is within 18 inches of another utility.
3. Class D bedding is not acceptable for new construction.
4. For flexible pipe (HDPE, PVC), use ASTM D2321 installation procedures instead of AASHTO bedding classes.
5. Shape bedding to support the lower quadrant of rigid pipe. Do not place rigid pipe on flat, unformed bedding — this creates point loading.

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|

4. All pipe connections shall be made with flexible boot seals to accommodate differential settlement.
5. Provide concrete collar (6-inch wide x 6-inch deep) around the frame at pavement surface where frame is not integral with the structure.

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|

1. Minimum 0.1-foot drop in invert elevation through manhole (inlet to outlet) for straight-through flow.

4. Maximum spacing between manholes: 400 feet for pipes 24 inches and smaller; 500 feet for pipes larger than 24 inches, or per local standards.
5. Do not locate manholes in wheel paths or pedestrian travel paths where avoidable. Adjust frame to match finished surface grade.

| 42-48 inches | 15 feet | 18 feet | KYTC Class III (D50 = 14 in) | 24 inches |
| 54-72 inches | 20 feet | 24 feet | Per hydraulic design | Per design |

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Concrete | 4,000 psi min; KYTC Class A |

3. Secure headwall to pipe with concrete encasement extending 24 inches beyond the wall face on the upstream side.
4. Provide a safety grate (trash rack) on outfall pipes larger than 24 inches where accessible to the public.
5. Endwalls on slopes steeper than 2:1 shall extend to the toe of slope or transition to riprap slope protection.

## Detail D-7: Pipe Outfall with Energy Dissipation

### Description

Energy dissipation at pipe outfalls to reduce velocity and prevent downstream erosion. Design based on outlet velocity and tailwater conditions.

### Design Criteria

| Outlet Velocity | Energy Dissipation Method |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Less than 4 fps | Riprap apron only (see Detail D-5) |
| 4-8 fps | Riprap apron with plunge pool |
| 8-15 fps | Concrete stilling basin (USBR Type IV or equivalent) |
| Greater than 15 fps | Engineered energy dissipator per hydraulic design |

### Plunge Pool Detail

| Parameter | Dimension |
|-----------|-----------|
| Pool length | 3x pipe diameter minimum |
| Pool width | 2x pipe diameter minimum |
| Pool depth below pipe invert | 1x pipe diameter |
| Riprap around pool perimeter | KYTC Class II or III |
| Riprap depth | 1.5x D50 minimum |

### Notes

1. Energy dissipation design shall prevent erosion velocities at the downstream limits of the riprap apron. Non-erosive velocity for bare soil: 3 fps; for established turf: 6 fps.
2. Outfalls discharging to streams, wetlands, or jurisdictional waters require compliance with KDOW and Army Corps of Engineers regulations.
3. Provide a stable outfall channel from the riprap apron to the receiving water body. Line channel with riprap or erosion control blanket if velocity exceeds 4 fps.
4. Monitor outfall condition during the first year after construction. Repair any scour immediately.

## Detail D-8: French Drain / Underdrain

### Description

Subsurface drainage system for intercepting and conveying groundwater, lowering the water table, or draining subgrade beneath pavements and structures.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Standard French Drain | Deep Interceptor Drain |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Trench width | 12 inches minimum | 18 inches minimum |
| Trench depth | 18-24 inches below grade | 36-60 inches (per design) |
| Pipe diameter | 4-inch perforated | 6-inch perforated |
| Pipe material | AASHTO M252 Type S (corrugated HDPE, smooth interior) | AASHTO M294 Type S or PVC SDR-35 perforated |
| Aggregate | KYTC No. 57 stone or No. 8 stone | KYTC No. 57 stone |
| Geotextile | Non-woven, AASHTO M288 Class 2 (wrap entire trench) | Non-woven, AASHTO M288 Class 2 |
| Minimum slope | 0.5% (1% preferred) | 0.5% (1% preferred) |

### Cross-Section (Bottom Up)

1. **Geotextile fabric** — line the trench bottom and sides with non-woven geotextile. Extend fabric up and over the top of the aggregate fill.
2. **Aggregate bedding** — 4-inch layer of clean, washed KYTC No. 57 stone.
3. **Perforated pipe** — place pipe with perforations facing down (6 o'clock position for single row perforations) or per manufacturer orientation.
4. **Aggregate surround** — fill trench with KYTC No. 57 stone to within 6 inches of finished grade.
5. **Geotextile closure** — fold the fabric over the top of the aggregate with a minimum 12-inch overlap.
6. **Topsoil cap** — 6-inch topsoil layer over the geotextile closure (or pavement section if under paving).

### Notes

1. French drains shall have a positive outlet to a storm drainage structure, ditch, or daylight point. Do not terminate a French drain with a dead end.
2. Provide cleanout risers (non-perforated pipe to surface, with cap) at the upstream end and at every change in direction.
3. Do not connect roof drains or surface drainage directly into the French drain — surface water overwhelms the subsurface capacity.
4. Use washed aggregate only. Unwashed stone introduces fines that clog the system.
5. Where French drains are located within 10 feet of building foundations, install on the exterior side of the foundation waterproofing and connect to the foundation drain system.

## Detail D-9: Bioretention Cell Cross-Section

### Description

Bioretention cell (rain garden) for stormwater quality treatment and volume reduction. Designed to filter runoff through an engineered soil media, remove pollutants, and infiltrate or underdrain excess water.

### Dimensions & Specifications (Bottom Up)

| Layer | Material | Depth | Specification |
|-------|----------|-------|---------------|
| Native subgrade | Existing soil (scarified) | - | Scarify 6 in if clay soils; do not compact |
| Geotextile (if underdrained) | Non-woven | - | AASHTO M288 Class 2; not required if infiltrating |
| Stone reservoir (optional) | ASTM No. 57 stone | 12 inches | For additional storage volume |
| Underdrain (if required) | 6-in perforated PVC (ASTM D3034) or HDPE | - | Slope 0.5% min to outlet; cleanouts at each end |
| Choking layer | ASTM No. 8 pea gravel | 3 inches | Prevents soil media migration into stone |
| Bioretention soil media (BSM) | Engineered mix | 18-24 inches | See mix specification below |
| Mulch layer | Shredded hardwood mulch | 3 inches | Do not use dyed mulch; replenish annually |
| Surface ponding zone | Open surface | 6-12 inches | Provides temporary storage; drains within 24-48 hours |
| Freeboard | Above maximum ponding | 6 inches minimum | Prevents overtopping during large storms |

### Bioretention Soil Media (BSM) Specification

| Component | Percentage by Volume | Notes |
|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| Sand (ASTM C33) | 60-70% | Washed concrete sand |
| Compost (US Composting Council STA-certified) | 20-30% | Mature, screened to 1/2 inch |
| Topsoil (sandy loam) | 10-20% | Low clay content (<10% clay fraction) |
| **Target infiltration rate** | **1-4 inches per hour** | Test per ASTM D3385 (double-ring infiltrometer) |
| **Target organic content** | **3-8%** | Per ASTM D2974 |

### Notes

1. Bioretention cells shall be sized to treat the Water Quality Volume (WQV) — typically the first 1.0 inch of runoff from the contributing impervious area, or per local jurisdiction requirements.
2. Provide a pretreatment forebay or filter strip upstream of the bioretention cell to remove coarse sediment. Forebay volume: 15-25% of WQV.
3. Provide an overflow structure (riser pipe or spillway) set at the maximum ponding elevation to convey larger storms to the downstream drainage system.
4. Underdrains are required where native soil infiltration rate is less than 0.5 inches per hour or where the seasonal high water table is within 2 feet of the cell bottom.
5. Do not locate bioretention cells within 10 feet of building foundations, 50 feet of septic systems, or 100 feet of drinking water wells.
6. Plant with deep-rooted native species tolerant of both wet and dry conditions. See JEDS Landscape Details for plant palette.
7. Maintenance: remove sediment from forebay annually; replenish mulch annually; replace BSM when infiltration drops below 1 inch per hour.

## Detail D-10: Detention Basin Outlet Structure

### Description

Multi-stage outlet structure for detention basins providing flow control for multiple design storm frequencies. Typically a concrete riser with orifice plates, weirs, and overflow provisions.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Riser pipe | 36-inch to 60-inch diameter precast concrete (ASTM C478) or cast-in-place |
| Riser wall thickness | 6 inches minimum |
| Anti-vortex plate | Steel plate welded to top of riser; extends 12 in beyond riser on each side |
| Trash rack | Galvanized steel or aluminum bar grate; openings no larger than 4 in x 4 in |
| Base/anti-flotation collar | Cast-in-place concrete; sized to resist buoyancy at maximum water elevation |
| Barrel pipe (outlet) | RCP per basin discharge design; ASTM C76, Class III min |
| Anti-seep collar(s) | Cast-in-place concrete; located at mid-point and near embankment face |

### Multi-Stage Outlet Configuration

| Stage | Control Device | Design Storm | Typical Location |
|-------|---------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Stage 1 — Water quality | Low-flow orifice (2-4 inch diameter) | WQV (first 1.0 in) | Bottom of riser; extended drawdown 24-48 hours |
| Stage 2 — Channel protection | Mid-level orifice or weir | 1-year, 24-hour storm | Mid-riser elevation |
| Stage 3 — Overbank flood | Upper orifice or weir | 2-yr and 10-yr storms | Upper riser elevation |
| Stage 4 — Extreme flood | Top of riser (weir) and emergency spillway | 25-yr and 100-yr storms | Top of riser + emergency spillway |

### Notes

1. All orifice sizes, weir lengths, and elevations shall be per the approved stormwater calculations. Do not field-modify orifice sizes without Engineer approval.
2. Provide orifice plates made of aluminum or stainless steel, bolted to the riser for removal and maintenance access.
3. Trash rack shall be removable for maintenance. Provide a locking mechanism to prevent unauthorized removal.
4. Outlet barrel pipe shall extend through the embankment with anti-seep collars. Minimum one collar at mid-embankment, one at 10 feet from the downstream face.
5. Provide a concrete cradle or anti-flotation collar at the riser base extending a minimum of 24 inches beyond the riser in all directions.
6. Access: provide a maintenance access path (10-ft minimum width, 15% max slope) from the basin perimeter road to the outlet structure.

## Detail D-11: Emergency Spillway

### Description

Emergency spillway for detention and retention basins to safely pass extreme storm events (100-year or greater) without overtopping or failing the embankment.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Minimum Requirement |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Spillway type | Vegetated earth channel (for low-hazard basins); concrete weir or armored channel (for significant/high hazard) |
| Spillway width | Per routing calculations; 10-ft minimum bottom width for earth channels |
| Spillway invert elevation | Set at or above the 100-year peak storage elevation; below top of embankment |
| Freeboard (top of embankment above spillway crest) | 1.0 foot minimum |
| Side slopes (earth spillway) | 3:1 (H:V) minimum |
| Armoring | Turf (low velocity); riprap or concrete (high velocity) |
| Velocity through spillway | Maximum 5 fps for vegetated earth; 8 fps for TRM-lined; per design for concrete |

### Notes

1. Emergency spillway shall be constructed in undisturbed native soil (cut through natural ground), not through fill embankment. If the spillway must traverse the embankment, provide concrete or riprap armoring for the full length.
2. Spillway alignment shall direct overflow away from the embankment toe, downstream structures, and adjacent properties.
3. Spillway must be capable of passing the 100-year event with the primary outlet assumed clogged (worst-case analysis) while maintaining minimum 1-foot freeboard.
4. For dams regulated by the Kentucky Division of Water (height > 25 feet or impoundment > 50 acre-feet), spillway design shall comply with KRS 151.250 and 401 KAR 4:030.
5. Vegetated spillways shall be established with permanent turf before the basin is operational. Protect with erosion control blanket during establishment.

## Detail D-12: Minimum Pipe Slopes Table

### Description

Minimum slopes to maintain self-cleansing velocity (2.5 fps minimum at design flow) for storm drainage pipe. Slopes assume Manning's n values of 0.013 for RCP, 0.012 for PVC, and 0.012 for smooth-interior HDPE.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Pipe Diameter (inches) | Minimum Slope — RCP (%) | Minimum Slope — PVC/HDPE (%) | Full-Flow Capacity at Min. Slope (cfs) |
|------------------------|------------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| 12 | 0.43 | 0.36 | 2.2 (RCP) |
| 15 | 0.31 | 0.26 | 3.8 |
| 18 | 0.24 | 0.20 | 6.0 |
| 21 | 0.19 | 0.16 | 8.7 |
| 24 | 0.16 | 0.13 | 12.1 |
| 30 | 0.11 | 0.09 | 21.2 |
| 36 | 0.08 | 0.07 | 33.8 |
| 42 | 0.06 | 0.05 | 50.3 |
| 48 | 0.05 | 0.04 | 70.7 |
| 54 | 0.04 | 0.04 | 95.6 |
| 60 | 0.04 | 0.03 | 125.0 |
| 72 | 0.03 | 0.02 | 200.5 |

### Notes

1. These are minimum slopes for self-cleansing velocity. Design slopes should be steeper where grade allows.
2. Where minimum slope cannot be maintained, increase pipe size to achieve the required capacity at a flatter slope. Do not reduce below the absolute minimums shown.
3. Maximum pipe velocity: 15 fps for RCP, 12 fps for PVC and HDPE (to prevent abrasion and joint separation).
4. Manning's equation: V = (1.486/n) x R^(2/3) x S^(1/2). Verify all pipe sizing with hydraulic calculations.
5. Slopes are percentage grades (rise per 100 feet of run). Convert to ft/ft by dividing by 100 for calculation purposes.

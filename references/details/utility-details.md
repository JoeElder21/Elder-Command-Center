## General Notes

1. All utility construction shall conform to the applicable standards of the serving utility, the local jurisdiction, and KYTC Standard Specifications where located within public right-of-way.
2. Contractor shall contact Kentucky 811 (dial 811 or 1-800-752-6007) a minimum of 2 working days before any excavation to locate existing underground utilities. Hand-expose all utilities within 24 inches of the proposed excavation.
3. All utility pipe and fittings shall be new, undamaged, and of domestic manufacture unless otherwise approved by the Engineer and the serving utility.
4. Contractor shall perform all pressure testing, leakage testing, bacteriological testing, and video inspection as required by the serving utility before connection to the existing system.
5. Trenching, shoring, and excavation safety shall comply with OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P. Trenches deeper than 5 feet require a competent person on site and trench protection (sloping, shoring, or shielding).
6. Do not backfill any utility trench until the installation has been inspected and approved by the serving utility and/or the Engineer.
7. All utility crossings shall maintain the minimum separation distances specified in Detail U-8. Where separations cannot be maintained, provide protection per the applicable standard.
8. As-built records: Contractor shall provide accurate horizontal and vertical locations of all installed utilities, fittings, valves, and services. Measurements to two permanent reference points at each fitting.

## Detail U-1: Water Main Trench Detail — Ductile Iron Pipe (DIP)

### Description

Standard trench cross-section for ductile iron water main installation. DIP is the standard material for water distribution mains in most Kentucky utilities.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Pipe material | Ductile Iron Pipe (DIP) per AWWA C151/A21.51 |
| Pipe class | Class 350 (minimum); Class 250 for 16-inch and larger (per utility) |
| Lining | Cement-morite lined per AWWA C104/A21.4 |
| Exterior coating | Asphaltic per AWWA C151; polyethylene encasement (AWWA C105/A21.5) in corrosive soils |
| Joint type | Push-on (AWWA C111/A21.11) or mechanical joint (AWWA C111) |
| Trench width | Pipe OD + 18 inches minimum (9 inches each side) |
| Minimum cover | 42 inches (3.5 ft) from top of pipe to finished grade |
| Maximum cover | 6 feet (unless otherwise approved) |
| Bedding | 6-inch compacted KYTC No. 8 or No. 57 stone |
| Initial backfill | KYTC No. 57 stone or select granular fill to 12 inches above top of pipe |
| Final backfill | Native soil, free of rocks > 6 in, in 8-in lifts compacted to 95% Std. Proctor |
| Under pavements | 98% Std. Proctor, top 12 inches |
| Tracer wire | 12-gauge insulated copper wire, attached to pipe; terminated in valve boxes |

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Ductile iron pipe | AWWA C151/A21.51; Class 350 min |
| Fittings | Ductile iron, AWWA C110/A21.10 (compact) or AWWA C153/A21.53 |
| Gaskets | SBR rubber per ASTM D2000 |
| Polyethylene encasement | 8-mil min, AWWA C105/A21.5 (in corrosive soils) |
| Tracer wire | 12-gauge THHN or equivalent insulated copper |
| Warning tape | Detectable metallic (blue) warning tape, 12 inches above pipe |

### Notes

1. Install tracer wire on all non-metallic pipe and fittings. Attach wire to the top of pipe with tape at 10-ft intervals. Bring wire to the surface at each valve box.
2. Install blue detectable warning tape 12 inches above the top of the pipe, centered over the pipe alignment.
3. Disinfect all new water mains per AWWA C651 before connection to the active system. Pass bacteriological testing (2 consecutive clean samples, 24 hours apart) before placing in service.
4. Pressure test per AWWA C600: test pressure = 1.5x working pressure or 150 psi, whichever is greater, for 2 hours. Allowable leakage per AWWA formula.
5. Restrain joints at all fittings, hydrants, valves, and dead ends per thrust restraint detail (Detail U-9).

## Detail U-2: Water Main Trench Detail — PVC C900

### Description

Standard trench for PVC water main (AWWA C900). PVC C900 is permitted by some Kentucky utilities as an alternative to DIP for distribution mains up to 12 inches.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Pipe material | PVC C900, DR 18 (Class 235) or DR 14 (Class 305) |
| Standard | AWWA C900; ASTM D2241 |
| Joint type | Push-on (elastomeric gasket); restrained joints at all fittings |
| Trench width | Pipe OD + 18 inches minimum |
| Minimum cover | 42 inches (3.5 ft) |
| Bedding | 6-inch compacted KYTC No. 8 or No. 57 stone |
| Initial backfill | Select granular fill or KYTC No. 57 to 12 inches above pipe |
| Final backfill | Native soil in 8-in lifts, 95% Std. Proctor |
| Tracer wire | 12-gauge insulated copper (required — PVC is non-metallic) |
| Warning tape | Detectable metallic blue tape, 12 inches above pipe |

### Notes

1. PVC C900 is more susceptible to damage from rocks and debris than DIP. Use clean granular bedding and initial backfill — no rocks in contact with pipe.
2. Maximum deflection at each joint: per manufacturer's recommendation (typically 3-5 degrees for push-on joints).
3. Do not store PVC pipe in direct sunlight for extended periods. UV degradation weakens the pipe wall.
4. Same testing and disinfection requirements as DIP (Detail U-1).
5. Verify PVC C900 is acceptable to the serving utility before specifying.

## Detail U-3: Sanitary Sewer Trench Detail — PVC SDR-35

### Description

Standard trench cross-section for gravity sanitary sewer installation using PVC SDR-35 pipe.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Pipe material | PVC SDR-35 per ASTM D3034 (4-15 in) or ASTM F679 (18-36 in) |
| Joint type | Elastomeric gasket (bell and spigot) per ASTM D3212 |
| Trench width | Pipe OD + 24 inches minimum |
| Minimum cover | 36 inches (3.0 ft) from top of pipe to finished grade |
| Bedding | 4-6 inches Class B granular (KYTC No. 57 or No. 8 stone) |
| Haunch zone | Granular material, hand-tamped to 70% of pipe diameter |
| Initial backfill | Granular or select fill to 12 inches above top of pipe, 90% Std. Proctor |
| Final backfill | Native soil (no rocks > 3 in), 8-in lifts, 95% Std. Proctor |
| Minimum slope | Per serving utility standards; see table below |
| Tracer wire | 12-gauge insulated copper (green), attached to pipe top |
| Warning tape | Detectable metallic green tape, 12 inches above pipe |

### Minimum Sewer Slopes (Gravity Flow)

| Pipe Diameter (inches) | Minimum Slope (%) | Velocity at Full Flow (fps) |
|------------------------|-------------------|-----------------------------|
| 4 (building sewer) | 2.00 | 2.3 |
| 6 | 0.60 | 2.2 |
| 8 | 0.40 | 2.3 |
| 10 | 0.28 | 2.2 |
| 12 | 0.22 | 2.2 |
| 15 | 0.15 | 2.2 |
| 18 | 0.12 | 2.4 |
| 21 | 0.10 | 2.5 |
| 24 | 0.08 | 2.3 |

### Notes

1. Minimum velocity at design flow: 2.0 fps (self-cleansing velocity). Maximum velocity: 10 fps.
2. Perform mandrel testing (ASTM F1743) on all installed PVC pipe. Mandrel diameter = 95% of pipe base inside diameter. Pass = mandrel passes through without stopping.
3. Perform low-pressure air testing per ASTM F1417 or infiltration/exfiltration testing per the serving utility standard.
4. Video inspect (CCTV) all sanitary sewer mains before acceptance. Provide video and written log to the utility and Engineer.
5. Service laterals (building sewers) shall connect to the main at the 10 o'clock or 2 o'clock position using a factory wye or saddle connection approved by the serving utility.

## Detail U-4: Fire Hydrant Installation

### Description

Standard fire hydrant installation on water distribution systems. Hydrant type, bury depth, and accessories per the serving utility's standards.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Hydrant type | Per serving utility standard; typically AWWA C502 dry-barrel |
| Connection to main | 6-inch branch with gate valve (AWWA C509 or C515) |
| Bury depth | Per hydrant manufacturer (match local frost depth; typically 42-48 inches in KY) |
| Hydrant nozzle configuration | One 4-1/2-inch steamer, two 2-1/2-inch hose connections (verify with fire marshal) |
| Operating nut | Pentagon; direction of opening per serving utility |
| Breakaway flange | Required; set at 1-2 inches above finished grade |
| Gravel drain sump | 2 ft x 2 ft x 2 ft, KYTC No. 57 stone (below drain ports) |
| Concrete thrust block | Behind hydrant tee and behind gate valve (see Detail U-9) |
| Minimum clearance to structures | 3 feet |
| Bollard protection | Required where exposed to vehicular traffic (3 bollards, 42-inch height, 4-inch steel pipe filled with concrete) |

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Fire hydrant | AWWA C502; UL/FM listed; per serving utility approved list |
| Gate valve | AWWA C509 (resilient seated) or C515; non-rising stem |
| Valve box | Cast iron, adjustable; "WATER" cast in lid |
| Hydrant lead pipe | 6-inch DIP per AWWA C151, Class 350 |
| Gravel drain | KYTC No. 57 stone |
| Thrust restraint | Concrete thrust blocks or restrained joints per Detail U-9 |

### Notes

1. Paint hydrant per serving utility color code. NFPA 291 color coding:
   - Light Blue: 1,500+ GPM
   - Green: 1,000-1,499 GPM
   - Orange: 500-999 GPM
   - Red: Less than 500 GPM
2. Hydrant spacing per local fire code — typically 300-500 feet in commercial areas, 500-800 feet in residential.
3. Set hydrant plumb and level with the lowest nozzle a minimum of 18 inches above finished grade.
4. Provide a minimum 3-foot clear zone around the hydrant (no landscaping, fencing, or obstructions). Coordinate clearance with the fire marshal.
5. Hydrant shall be flow-tested after installation; results documented for the fire marshal and serving utility.
6. Do not install hydrant on a dead-end main less than 6 inches in diameter.

## Detail U-5: Water Meter and Service Connection

### Description

Water service connection from the distribution main to the water meter, including corporation stop, service line, curb stop, meter setter, and meter.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Residential (3/4-inch to 1-inch) | Commercial (2-inch and larger) |
|-----------|--------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Corporation stop | Brass; tapped into main under pressure | Brass or ductile iron; tapped or hot-tapped |
| Service line | Type K copper (3/4-1 in) or PEX per utility | DIP or PVC C900 per utility |
| Curb stop | Brass ball valve with curb box | Gate valve (AWWA C509) with valve box |
| Meter setter (yoke) | Per utility standard; preset for meter size | Per utility standard |
| Meter | Provided and installed by serving utility | Provided and installed by serving utility |
| Meter box/vault | Per utility standard; cast iron lid with "WATER" | Concrete vault per utility detail |
| Minimum cover | 42 inches (frost protection) | 42 inches |

### Notes

1. Service line shall be a single continuous length from the corporation stop to the curb stop — no joints in the service line between main and meter.
2. Meter location per serving utility requirements. Typically within 2 feet of the right-of-way line or at the property line.
3. Provide a check valve and/or backflow prevention assembly downstream of the meter per local plumbing code and utility requirements. Double-check valve assembly (DCVA) for commercial; reduced pressure zone (RPZ) assembly for fire sprinkler connections and high-hazard uses.
4. Coordinate meter size with the plumbing engineer to ensure adequate flow and pressure for the building's domestic and fire protection demands.
5. Avoid routing the service line under driveways, structures, or other permanent improvements.

## Detail U-6: Sanitary Sewer Manhole

### Description

Standard precast concrete sanitary sewer manhole for changes in pipe direction, pipe size, pipe slope, or for access to the sewer system at prescribed intervals.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Standard Manhole | Drop Manhole |
|-----------|-----------------|--------------|
| Inside diameter | 48 inches (pipes up to 24 in) | 48-60 inches |
| Wall thickness | 6 inches minimum | 6 inches minimum |
| Cone section | Eccentric, reducing to 24-in frame | Same |
| Base | Precast base with integral invert channel | Same, with drop pipe (outside or inside) |
| Invert channel | Smooth, formed half-pipe; match incoming pipe size and slope | Smooth invert at outlet pipe elevation |
| Bench | Sloped minimum 1 in/ft toward channel | Same |
| Frame and cover | 24-inch diameter; watertight (ASTM C478); "SANITARY SEWER" cast in lid | Same |
| Steps | Polypropylene (OSHA-compliant); 12 in O.C. | Same |
| Joints | Butyl rubber sealant (ASTM C990) between sections | Same |
| Drop pipe (drop MH only) | N/A | DIP or PVC; exterior or interior; with energy dissipation fitting at base |

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Precast concrete | ASTM C478; 4,000 psi min |
| Joint sealant | ASTM C990 butyl rubber |
| Frame and cover | AASHTO M105 (ductile iron); watertight; bolted or gasketed |
| Pipe boots | Flexible rubber (Kor-N-Seal or approved equal) |
| Invert coating | Epoxy coating per ASTM C990 (if required by utility for H2S protection) |
| External waterproofing | Bituminous coating per KYTC standard (below groundwater table) |

### Notes

1. Maximum manhole spacing: 400 feet for pipes 15 inches and smaller; 500 feet for pipes 18 inches and larger, per 10 States Standards and local utility requirements.
2. Minimum 0.1-foot drop through the manhole (inlet invert to outlet invert) for straight-through flows.
3. For drops greater than 24 inches between inlet and outlet pipe inverts, provide a drop manhole with an external or internal drop pipe.
4. Manholes shall be vacuum-tested per ASTM C1244 before backfilling. Allowable vacuum drop: no more than 1 inch of mercury in 2 minutes (for 48-inch diameter).
5. In high-groundwater areas, install external joint wrap (rubber membrane) and coat exterior walls with bituminous waterproofing.
6. Manhole lids in traffic areas shall be traffic-rated (H-20 minimum). Adjust frame to finished grade with a maximum of two concrete grade rings (8 inches total height).

## Detail U-7: Sanitary Cleanout

### Description

Cleanout fitting at the end of sanitary sewer laterals and at changes in direction for access and maintenance. Required at the building connection and at specified intervals along the building sewer.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Cleanout size | Same diameter as the service lateral (4-inch or 6-inch typical) |
| Fitting type | Wye with 1/8 bend (45-degree) or tee (horizontal) per plumbing code |
| Riser pipe | Same material as lateral (PVC Schedule 40 from wye to grade) |
| Cap | Threaded or gasketed PVC cleanout plug, flush with finished grade |
| Cleanout box | Cast iron or PVC box with "SEWER" or "S" marked lid; set flush with grade |
| Maximum spacing | 75 feet for 4-inch pipe; 100 feet for 6-inch pipe (or per local code) |
| Required locations | Within 3 feet of building foundation; at each change in direction > 45 degrees; at property line (per utility) |

### Notes

1. Set cleanout riser plumb and at an angle that allows insertion of a sewer cleaning rod in the direction of flow.
2. Cleanout caps shall be accessible — do not cover with pavement, landscaping, or structures.
3. Where cleanouts are located in vehicular traffic areas, use a traffic-rated frame and cover in lieu of the standard cleanout box.
4. Two-way cleanouts are recommended at the building connection to allow cleaning upstream (toward the building) and downstream (toward the main).

## Detail U-8: Utility Crossing Detail and Minimum Separation Distances

### Description

Requirements for utility crossings and minimum horizontal and vertical separation distances between parallel utilities. Separation distances are based on the 10 States Standards (GLUMRB), Kentucky DOW regulations, and KYTC requirements.

### Minimum Separation Distances — Parallel Utilities

| Utility A | Utility B | Minimum Horizontal Separation | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------------------|-------|
| Water main | Sanitary sewer | 10 feet (edge to edge) | Per 10 States Standards / 401 KAR 8:100 |
| Water main | Storm sewer | 10 feet (edge to edge) | Recommended; per local standards |
| Water main | Gas main | 3-5 feet (per gas utility) | Verify with gas provider |
| Water main | Electric conduit | 3-5 feet | Verify with electric provider |
| Water main | Telecom conduit | 3-5 feet | Verify with telecom provider |
| Sanitary sewer | Storm sewer | 3-5 feet | Per local standards |
| Sanitary sewer | Gas main | 3-5 feet | Verify with gas provider |
| Sanitary sewer | Building foundation | 3 feet minimum | Per plumbing code |
| Gas main | Electric conduit | 12 inches (per gas utility) | Verify with providers |
| Electric conduit | Telecom conduit | 12 inches | Verify with providers |

### Minimum Separation at Crossings (Vertical)

| Crossing Condition | Minimum Vertical Separation | Special Requirements |
|-------------------|----------------------------|---------------------|
| Water main OVER sanitary sewer | 18 inches (outside to outside) | Water main must be above sewer at the crossing |
| Water main UNDER sanitary sewer | 18 inches + special protection | Sewer pipe: one full joint length of DIP or encased in concrete both sides of crossing (min. 10 ft centered on water main) |
| Water main crossing storm sewer | 18 inches (outside to outside) | Water main should be above storm sewer where possible |
| Sewer crossing above water (not permitted — relocate) | N/A | Relocate to achieve water-over-sewer; if impossible, encase sewer in concrete for 10 ft centered on crossing |

### Encasement Requirements (When Separation Cannot Be Met)

1. Where the required horizontal or vertical separation cannot be maintained, encase the lower-quality utility (sewer) in concrete or steel casing for a minimum of 10 feet centered on the crossing.
2. Concrete encasement: 6-inch minimum concrete surround (4,000 psi) for the full encasement length.
3. Steel casing: minimum 1/4-inch wall thickness; diameter 4 inches larger than the carrier pipe; sealed at both ends with modular rubber seals and end plate.
4. Obtain a variance from the Kentucky Division of Water (KDOW) if the 10-foot horizontal separation between water and sewer cannot be maintained.

### Notes

1. All separation distances are measured edge-to-edge (outside of pipe to outside of pipe), not center-to-center.
2. Where utilities share a common trench, maintain the minimum horizontal separation with an earthen separation (no shared bedding between different utility types).
3. Verify all separation requirements with the specific serving utility — some providers have more stringent requirements than the minimums shown.
4. Gas utility crossings under roadways typically require a casing. Coordinate with the gas provider for casing requirements.

## Detail U-9: Thrust Restraint

### Description

Thrust restraint at water main fittings, hydrants, valves, and dead ends to resist hydraulic thrust forces that tend to push fittings apart at unbalanced pressure points.

### Thrust Block Dimensions (Typical — Verify with Design)

| Fitting Type | Thrust Direction | Bearing Area Required (SF per 100 psi) — 6-in | Bearing Area — 8-in | Bearing Area — 12-in |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 90-degree bend | Bisects angle | 2.0 | 3.5 | 7.8 |
| 45-degree bend | Bisects angle | 1.1 | 1.9 | 4.2 |
| 22.5-degree bend | Bisects angle | 0.5 | 1.0 | 2.2 |
| Tee (branch) | Along branch | 1.4 | 2.5 | 5.5 |
| Dead end / cap | Along pipe | 1.4 | 2.5 | 5.5 |
| Reducer | Toward smaller pipe | Varies by size diff. | Varies | Varies |

*Note: Bearing areas are approximate for typical soil bearing capacity (1,500 psf). Adjust for actual soil conditions per geotechnical report. Calculate per AWWA M23.*

### Thrust Block Construction

1. Pour concrete thrust blocks directly against undisturbed native soil. Do not pour against fill or excavated trench walls.
2. Concrete: 3,000 psi minimum.
3. Place polyethylene sheet between the pipe/fitting and the concrete to allow future disassembly if needed.
4. Block shall bear against the pipe fitting, not the pipe barrel.
5. Allow concrete to cure 7 days minimum before pressurizing the main.

### Restrained Joint Alternative

Where thrust blocks are impractical (rock, high water table, limited space), use restrained joints:

| Fitting Type | Restrained Joint Length Each Side |
|-------------|----------------------------------|
| 90-degree bend | 40 feet (8-in pipe); verify per AWWA M23 |
| 45-degree bend | 25 feet |
| Tee | 30 feet (on branch and each side of run) |
| Dead end | 40 feet |
| Hydrant | 30 feet back from hydrant tee |

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Thrust block concrete | 3,000 psi min; unreinforced |
| Polyethylene separator | 6-mil min |
| Restrained joint system | Megalug (EBAA Iron), Uni-Flange, or approved equal; per AWWA C110/C111 |

### Notes

1. Do not backfill over thrust blocks until concrete has cured a minimum of 7 days and the line has been pressure-tested.
2. At dead ends and blow-offs, install a thrust block AND a restrainer gland for redundancy.
3. In corrosive soils, wrap thrust blocks with polyethylene and provide cathodic protection per the serving utility's requirements.

## Detail U-10: Trench Backfill and Compaction Notes

### Description

General backfill and compaction requirements for all utility trenches. These requirements supplement the specific pipe-material details (Details U-1 through U-3).

### Compaction Requirements by Zone

| Trench Zone | Material | Compaction | Method |
|-------------|----------|------------|--------|
| Bedding | Granular (No. 57 or No. 8 stone) | Hand-tamp in 4-in lifts | Manual or walk-behind vibrator |
| Haunch (pipe springline to 70% up) | Granular | Hand-tamp | Manual tamper; no heavy equipment |
| Initial backfill (pipe to 12 in above top) | Granular or select fill | 90% Std. Proctor | Walk-behind compactor |
| Intermediate backfill (12 in above pipe to 24 in below surface) | Native soil, select fill, or granular | 95% Std. Proctor | Vibratory plate or trench roller |
| Top backfill (under pavement, top 12 in) | KYTC DGA or select fill | 98% Std. Proctor | Vibratory roller |
| Top backfill (under landscape, top 6 in) | Topsoil | Lightly compacted | Hand tamper; seed/sod immediately |

### Notes

1. Compaction testing frequency: one test per 200 linear feet per lift, or one test per 100 linear feet under pavements. Minimum one test per trench segment between structures.
2. Do not use jetting (water flooding) as a compaction method in utility trenches.
3. Flowable fill (controlled density fill, CDF) may be used as an alternative to granular backfill where approved by the Engineer. CDF strength: 50-150 psi (removable) or per design.
4. In rock trenches, provide a minimum 6-inch cushion of granular bedding between rock and the pipe. Do not place pipe directly on rock.
5. Restore all pavement, curb, sidewalk, landscape, and turf disturbed by trenching operations to pre-construction condition or better.

## Detail U-11: Utility Connection to Existing System

### Description

General notes for connecting new utility mains and services to existing active systems. Connections to active pressure mains (water) and gravity systems (sanitary sewer) require coordination with the serving utility.

### Water Main Connections

1. Notify the serving utility a minimum of 48 hours before the planned connection. Many utilities require their crew or inspector to be present.
2. Line stops and wet taps shall be performed by qualified personnel using appropriate equipment. Do not cut or tap an active water main without utility approval.
3. Disinfect and pressure-test all new pipe before connection to the active system. Provide test results to the utility.
4. After connection, flush the new main at a minimum velocity of 2.5 fps until water runs clear. Collect bacteriological samples at the far end of the new main.
5. Valve all new mains with an isolation valve (gate or butterfly) at the point of connection.
6. Provide a blow-off assembly at the dead end of all new mains for flushing and testing.

### Sanitary Sewer Connections

1. New sewer main connections to existing manholes: core-drill the manhole wall and install a flexible boot seal (Kor-N-Seal or approved equal). Form and pour a new invert channel section to match the new pipe.
2. New service lateral connections: use a factory wye fitting where possible. Where connecting to an existing main, use an approved saddle connection per the serving utility's requirements.
3. Do not make connections during wet-weather flow conditions when flow in the existing main exceeds 50% of pipe capacity.
4. Plug or cap all new connections immediately after installation if the building sewer is not connected. Prevent infiltration and inflow into the new system.
5. Provide smoke testing or dye testing of the new system before acceptance if required by the serving utility.

### Notes

1. Document all connections with photographs and as-built measurements.
2. Restore all disturbed pavement, curb, sidewalk, and landscape to pre-construction condition after connection.
3. Remove all temporary plugs, blind flanges, and blow-off assemblies after final connection, unless designated as permanent.

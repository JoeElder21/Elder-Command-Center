## General Notes

1. All landscape work shall conform to the latest edition of the American Standard for Nursery Stock (ANSI Z60.1) and the project landscape construction documents.
2. All plant material shall be nursery-grown, healthy, vigorous, and free of disease, pests, and mechanical damage. Plants shall be true to species and cultivar specified. No substitutions without written approval from the Landscape Architect.
3. All plants shall be hardy to USDA Zone 6b (Kentucky) with documented cold hardiness to -5 degrees F minimum.
4. Contractor shall verify all underground utility locations before planting. Do not plant trees within 10 feet horizontally of underground utilities without root barrier (see Detail L-6). Do not excavate within 24 inches of marked utilities without hand digging.
5. All planting areas shall receive a minimum of 6 inches of topsoil (amended per soil test) unless existing topsoil is suitable as determined by soil test results.
6. All plant beds shall receive 3 inches of organic mulch after planting. Mulch shall not be placed against tree trunks or shrub crowns (see Detail L-9).
7. The Contractor shall guarantee all plant material for a minimum of one (1) year from the date of Substantial Completion. Replace dead or failing plants at no additional cost during the warranty period.
8. Irrigation system (if specified) shall be installed and operational before planting.
9. All plants shall be watered thoroughly at the time of planting and maintained on a regular watering schedule through the establishment period (first two growing seasons).

## Detail L-1: Shade Tree Planting — Balled and Burlapped (B&B)

### Description

Standard planting detail for deciduous shade trees supplied as balled and burlapped (B&B) stock. Minimum caliper: 2.5 inches measured 6 inches above the root flare. This detail also applies to large evergreen trees supplied as B&B.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Planting hole diameter | 3x root ball diameter |
| Planting hole depth | Equal to root ball depth (set root flare at or 1-2 inches above finished grade) |
| Root ball handling | Do not lift by trunk; carry/lift by root ball only |
| Burlap treatment | Remove top 1/3 of burlap and all wire basket after tree is set plumb and at correct depth |
| Twine/rope | Remove all twine from trunk and root ball |
| Backfill mix | Existing excavated soil (unamended) or 75% native / 25% compost if native soil is poor |
| Backfill compaction | Lightly firm in 6-inch lifts; do not over-compact |
| Watering saucer | 4-inch high berm at edge of planting hole to retain water |
| Mulch ring | 3-inch depth, 36-inch diameter minimum; keep mulch 3-6 inches away from trunk |
| Staking | Per Detail L-7 (2-stake or 3-stake system) |
| Trunk protection | Tree wrap (spiral wrap) on thin-barked species from November through March for first 2 years |

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Tree stock | ANSI Z60.1 compliant; 2.5-inch caliper minimum; single straight leader for street/shade trees |
| Mulch | Shredded hardwood bark; undyed; aged minimum 6 months |
| Stakes | 2-inch x 2-inch hardwood or metal T-posts; 5-foot length |
| Staking ties | Wide nylon or rubber tree ties; no wire or rope against bark |
| Soil amendment (if needed) | Composted organic material per USCC STA standards |

### Notes

1. Identify the root flare before planting. If the root flare is buried below the top of the root ball, excavate excess soil to expose it. Set root flare at or 1-2 inches above finished grade.
2. Score the sides of the planting hole with a shovel to roughen glazed surfaces (especially in clay soils) and promote root penetration.
3. Do not add gravel to the bottom of the planting hole — this creates a perched water table.
4. Remove all nursery tags, labels, and identification tags after planting records are made.
5. Water immediately after planting: saturate the entire root ball and backfill area. Provide a minimum of 1 inch of water per week during the first growing season (supplemental irrigation during dry periods).

## Detail L-2: Shade Tree Planting — Container Grown

### Description

Planting detail for deciduous shade trees supplied in containers. Container trees require additional root treatment to prevent circling roots.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Planting hole diameter | 3x container diameter |
| Planting hole depth | Equal to root mass depth (root flare at or 1-2 inches above grade) |
| Root treatment | Remove container entirely; inspect root ball for circling roots |
| Circling root treatment | Score root ball vertically in 4 places, 1-inch deep; spread or prune any circling roots larger than 1/4-inch diameter |
| Backfill, mulch, staking | Same as Detail L-1 |

### Notes

1. Container-grown trees are more susceptible to circling (girdling) roots than B&B stock. Thorough root treatment is critical to long-term tree health.
2. If the root ball disintegrates when the container is removed, handle as a bare-root tree: spread roots radially in the planting hole over a mound of backfill soil.
3. Planting season for container stock is more flexible than B&B — spring through early fall is acceptable if supplemental watering is provided.

## Detail L-3: Ornamental Tree Planting

### Description

Planting detail for small ornamental and understory trees (mature height under 25 feet), including multi-stem specimens.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Minimum caliper | 1.5 inches (single stem) or per specification |
| Multi-stem requirement | Minimum 3 stems, 6-foot height minimum |
| Planting hole | 3x root ball diameter; depth equal to root ball depth |
| Root flare | At or 1-2 inches above finished grade |
| Burlap/wire treatment | Same as Detail L-1 (B&B) or Detail L-2 (container) |
| Staking | 2-stake system for single-stem (remove after 1 year); multi-stem may not require staking |
| Mulch ring | 3-inch depth, 24-36 inches diameter minimum; 3-6 inches away from trunk(s) |

### Notes

1. Ornamental trees are often planted near buildings, walks, and utilities. Verify mature canopy spread does not conflict with structures, sight triangles, or overhead utilities.
2. Multi-stem ornamental trees shall have stems originating below the first major branch union — not a single trunk that was topped or cut back to produce multiple leaders.
3. Low-branching ornamental trees in vehicular areas shall be limbed up to provide a minimum 7-foot clearance over sidewalks and 14 feet over vehicular drives at maturity.

## Detail L-4: Evergreen Tree Planting

### Description

Planting detail for evergreen trees (coniferous and broadleaf evergreen) used for screening, windbreaks, and year-round visual interest.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Minimum size | 6-foot height for conifers; 5-foot height for broadleaf evergreens |
| Planting hole diameter | 2-3x root ball diameter |
| Planting hole depth | Equal to root ball depth (set slightly high — 1-2 inches above grade) |
| Burlap/wire treatment | Same as Detail L-1 |
| Staking | Generally not required for conifers unless site is very windy; guy wire system for large specimens (8 ft+ height) |
| Mulch ring | 3-inch depth, 36-inch diameter minimum; keep mulch 3-6 inches from trunk |
| Spacing for screening | 8-12 feet O.C. for large conifers (spruce, pine); 5-8 feet O.C. for arborvitae; stagger rows for opacity |

### Notes

1. Evergreens are less tolerant of deep planting than deciduous trees. Set root flare 1-2 inches above finished grade to prevent crown rot.
2. For screening applications, specify minimum size at installation that provides immediate visual screening (typically 6-8 feet for conifers).
3. Evergreen trees planted on the south or west side of buildings should be set back a minimum distance equal to the mature canopy spread to prevent foundation shading conflicts.
4. Anti-desiccant spray recommended for broadleaf evergreens (holly, magnolia) planted after October 1 in Kentucky. Apply per manufacturer's directions.
5. Provide wind protection (burlap screen or snow fence) for evergreens planted in exposed locations during the first winter.

## Detail L-5: Shrub Planting

### Description

Planting detail for deciduous and evergreen shrubs in mass plantings and individual specimen locations.

### Dimensions & Specifications

**Mass Planting**

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Minimum size | #3 container (3-gallon) for most shrubs; #5 (5-gallon) for specimen or focal plantings |
| Planting hole | 2x root ball diameter; depth equal to root ball depth |
| Spacing | Per plant spacing table (Detail L-14); based on mature spread |
| Bed preparation | Rototill or excavate entire bed area to 12-inch depth; amend with 3 inches of compost incorporated |
| Mulch | 3-inch depth across entire bed; keep mulch 2-3 inches away from plant crowns |
| Edging | Steel, aluminum, or plastic edging per Detail L-10 |

**Individual Specimen**

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Minimum size | #5 container (5-gallon) or larger |
| Planting hole | 3x root ball diameter |
| Mulch ring | 3-inch depth, 24-inch diameter minimum |

### Materials

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Shrub stock | ANSI Z60.1 compliant; container-grown or B&B |
| Mulch | Shredded hardwood bark; undyed; 3-inch depth |
| Soil amendment | Composted organic material; 3 inches incorporated into top 12 inches of soil |
| Pre-emergent herbicide (optional) | Granular, labeled for use around ornamentals; apply per manufacturer rate after mulching |

### Notes

1. For container-grown shrubs: remove from container, loosen outer roots, and butterfly or score the root ball if root-bound.
2. For mass plantings: lay out all plants in the bed before digging holes to verify spacing and composition.
3. Set the top of the root ball flush with or 1/2 inch above the mulch surface. Do not bury the crown of the plant.
4. Group plants with similar water requirements together to facilitate efficient irrigation.

## Detail L-6: Root Barrier Installation

### Description

Root barrier installation between tree plantings and adjacent pavements, foundations, or utilities to prevent root damage. Required when trees are planted within 10 feet of hardscape, underground utilities, or building foundations.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Barrier material | Linear low-density polyethylene (LLDPE) or polypropylene; 24-inch depth minimum (36-inch preferred near foundations) |
| Barrier thickness | 60-mil minimum |
| Barrier length | Extend 5 feet beyond the canopy dripline on each side of the tree (or the full length of the improvement being protected) |
| Installation depth | Top of barrier flush with or 1 inch above finished grade; bottom at 24-36 inches below grade |
| Distance from tree | 5-foot minimum from tree trunk (closer restricts root development) |
| Overlap at joints | 12 inches minimum; sealed with manufacturer's adhesive |

### Notes

1. Root barriers redirect roots downward — they do not stop root growth. The barrier must be deep enough (24-36 inches) to redirect roots below the zone of potential damage.
2. Do not install root barriers in a closed circle around the tree — this will girdle the roots and kill the tree. Install as a linear panel between the tree and the improvement to be protected.
3. Root barriers are not a substitute for proper species selection. Choose species with non-aggressive root systems near infrastructure.
4. Verify root barrier material is UV-stabilized for the exposed top edge.

## Detail L-7: Tree Staking

### Description

Staking systems to stabilize newly planted trees during the establishment period. Staking prevents wind throw and root ball movement until new roots anchor the tree.

### 2-Stake System (Standard — Trees up to 4-inch Caliper)

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Number of stakes | 2, placed perpendicular to prevailing wind direction |
| Stake material | 2-inch x 2-inch hardwood or 1-1/4-inch steel T-posts |
| Stake length | 5 feet (driven 24 inches into undisturbed soil outside the root ball) |
| Tie material | Wide (2-inch minimum) flexible nylon or rubber strap |
| Tie height on trunk | 2/3 of tree height above ground |
| Slack | Allow 2-3 inches of trunk movement (slight flex promotes caliper growth) |
| Duration | Remove after one growing season (12 months maximum) |

### 3-Stake System (Large Trees — 4-inch Caliper and Larger, or Windy Sites)

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Number of stakes | 3, equally spaced (120 degrees apart) |
| Stake material | 2-inch x 2-inch hardwood or 2-inch diameter metal posts |
| Stake length | 6 feet (driven 30 inches into undisturbed soil) |
| Guy wire | 12-gauge galvanized wire with hose or tubing sleeve where wire contacts tree |
| Flag | High-visibility flagging tape on each wire (pedestrian safety) |
| Duration | Remove after 1-2 growing seasons |

### Notes

1. Never attach wire, rope, or cable directly to bark — always use a protective sleeve or wide strap.
2. If ties are not removed within the specified period, they will girdle the trunk and kill the tree. Schedule removal.
3. Not all trees require staking. Small ornamentals, multi-stem trees, and trees in protected locations may not need staking. Stake only when conditions warrant it (wind exposure, loose soil, large canopy).
4. Check staking tension quarterly and adjust as the tree grows. Re-tighten or replace any loose or broken ties.

## Detail L-8: Tree Protection / Fencing During Construction

### Description

Protective fencing around existing trees to be preserved during construction. Tree protection prevents soil compaction, root damage, bark injury, and grade changes within the critical root zone (CRZ).

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Critical root zone (CRZ) radius | 1 foot of radius per 1 inch of trunk diameter (measured at 4.5 ft above grade / DBH) |
| Fence location | At CRZ perimeter; minimum 6 feet from trunk if CRZ calculation results in less |
| Fence type | 4-foot minimum height orange construction fence on metal T-posts |
| Post spacing | 8 feet maximum O.C. |
| Signage | "TREE PROTECTION ZONE — DO NOT ENTER, DIG, STORE MATERIALS, OR PARK" at each fence face |

### Prohibitions Within the CRZ

1. No excavation, trenching, or grade changes (cut or fill) without Landscape Architect approval.
2. No storage of materials, soil, equipment, or debris.
3. No vehicular or equipment traffic.
4. No attachment of signs, wires, or fencing to the tree.
5. No concrete washout, fuel storage, or chemical application.

### Notes

1. Tree protection fencing shall be installed before any land disturbance activity begins and remain in place until construction is substantially complete.
2. Where construction must occur within the CRZ (utility crossings, grading transitions), use the following mitigation:
   - Hand-dig only (no mechanical excavation within CRZ)
   - Tunnel under roots larger than 2 inches in diameter
   - Clean-cut any severed roots with a sharp saw — do not tear
   - Apply wound sealant to clean-cut root surfaces
   - Backfill immediately with the original soil; water thoroughly
3. Trees damaged during construction (bark wounds, root loss exceeding 25% of CRZ, broken scaffold branches) may be subject to replacement at the Contractor's expense per the tree replacement value formula.
4. For high-value trees, engage a certified arborist (ISA) for a pre-construction tree assessment and post-construction monitoring plan.

## Detail L-9: Mulch Ring

### Description

Proper mulch application around trees and in shrub beds to conserve moisture, suppress weeds, moderate soil temperature, and improve soil organic matter.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Trees | Shrub Beds |
|-----------|-------|------------|
| Mulch depth | 3 inches | 3 inches |
| Mulch ring diameter | 36 inches minimum from trunk (larger is better; to dripline if feasible) | Full bed coverage |
| Clearance from trunk | 3-6 inches (NO mulch touching bark) | 2-3 inches from plant crown |
| Mulch material | Shredded hardwood bark; aged; undyed | Shredded hardwood bark; aged; undyed |
| Replenish schedule | Annually (spring); add 1-2 inches to maintain 3-inch total depth | Annually (spring) |

### Prohibited Practices

1. **No volcano mulching.** Do not mound mulch against the tree trunk in a cone or volcano shape. This holds moisture against the bark, promotes decay, encourages girdling roots above the root flare, and provides habitat for rodents that gnaw bark.
2. Maximum mulch depth is 4 inches. Excessive mulch depth (over 4 inches) reduces oxygen to roots and creates anaerobic conditions.
3. Do not use dyed mulch (red, black) on JEDS projects unless specifically approved by the Landscape Architect.
4. Do not use landscape fabric beneath mulch in planting beds — fabric prevents organic matter incorporation and creates maintenance problems as mulch decomposes on top of the fabric.

### Notes

1. Acceptable mulch materials: shredded hardwood bark, pine bark nuggets (in beds only, not on slopes), and composted wood chips.
2. Unacceptable mulch materials: cypress mulch (unsustainable), rubber mulch (in planting areas), stone/gravel (in planting areas — restricts soil improvement).
3. For slopes, use shredded bark (interlocks and resists washing); do not use nuggets or chips on slopes.

## Detail L-10: Landscape Bed Edging

### Description

Edging installation at the perimeter of landscape beds to separate mulch/planting areas from turf, pavement, or other adjacent surfaces.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Edging Type | Material | Depth | Thickness | Staking |
|-------------|----------|-------|-----------|---------|
| Steel edging | 14-gauge galvanized steel | 4 inches below grade / 1 inch above | 1/4 inch | Steel stakes at 36 in O.C. and at joints |
| Aluminum edging | Commercial-grade aluminum | 4 inches below grade / 1 inch above | 1/8 inch | Aluminum stakes at 36 in O.C. |
| Plastic edging (commercial) | High-density polyethylene | 4 inches below grade / flush to 1 inch above | Varies | Plastic stakes at 24 in O.C. |
| Plastic edging (residential) | Not recommended for JEDS commercial projects | - | - | - |
| Concrete mow strip | Cast-in-place or precast concrete | 4 inches deep | 4-6 inches wide | N/A (monolithic) |

### Notes

1. Steel edging is the standard for JEDS commercial projects — it provides a clean, durable, permanent edge.
2. Edging shall follow a smooth, continuous line. Avoid jagged transitions. Use factory radius pieces for curves.
3. Top of edging shall be flush with or slightly above adjacent turf (1 inch max) to prevent mulch migration.
4. Overlap edging joints a minimum of 6 inches and secure with a coupler or stake at the overlap.
5. Concrete mow strips are recommended where beds abut high-maintenance turf areas to reduce string-trimming.

## Detail L-11: Irrigation Sleeve

### Description

Irrigation sleeves (conduit) installed beneath paved surfaces to allow future irrigation pipe routing without cutting pavement. Install during initial site construction before paving.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Sleeve material | Schedule 40 PVC or HDPE |
| Sleeve diameter | 4-inch minimum (6-inch preferred for main line sleeves) |
| Minimum cover | 18 inches below finished pavement grade |
| Quantity | One sleeve per irrigation zone crossing; minimum 2 sleeves at each major pavement crossing |
| End treatment | Cap both ends with removable PVC caps; install locating wire (12-gauge tracer) attached to each sleeve |
| Marking | Install a marker post or flush marker at each end labeled "IRRIGATION SLEEVE" |

### Notes

1. Install sleeves during rough grading or utility installation — before aggregate base and pavement.
2. Pull a nylon pull-rope through each sleeve during installation for future pipe pulling.
3. Record all sleeve locations on the as-built drawings with dimensions to two permanent reference points.
4. Oversleeve (2 sleeves) at critical crossings to provide redundancy and separate mainline from lateral runs.

## Detail L-12: Topsoil Placement and Preparation

### Description

Requirements for topsoil placement in landscape areas, including amended planting beds and seeded/sodded lawn areas.

### Dimensions & Specifications

| Application | Topsoil Depth | Amendment | Preparation |
|-------------|---------------|-----------|-------------|
| Planting beds (trees and shrubs) | 6 inches minimum (12 inches preferred) | 3 inches compost incorporated to 12-inch depth | Rototill to 12 inches; rake to smooth, uniform surface |
| Seeded lawn areas | 4 inches minimum | Per soil test; typically 10-20-20 fertilizer at 400 lbs/acre | Disc or rototill to 4-inch depth; final grade with landscape rake |
| Sodded lawn areas | 4 inches minimum | Per soil test | Fine grade to within 1/2 inch of proposed grade (sod adds ~1 inch) |
| Bioretention / rain garden | Per BSM specification (Detail D-9) | Engineered soil media | Per Detail D-9 |

### Topsoil Quality Standards

| Parameter | Requirement | Test Method |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Organic content | 3-8% | ASTM D2974 |
| pH | 6.0-7.0 | ASTM D4972 |
| Sand content | 40-60% | ASTM D422 |
| Clay content | 10-25% | ASTM D422 |
| Free of debris | No rocks > 1 inch, no roots, no trash | Visual inspection |
| Weed seed | Free of noxious weed seeds and rhizomes | Certification from supplier |

### Notes

1. Submit a soil test from the topsoil source for approval before delivery. Imported topsoil shall meet the quality standards above.
2. Do not place topsoil on frozen or saturated subgrade. Do not work or compact wet topsoil.
3. Scarify the subgrade surface before topsoil placement to prevent a compacted interface layer.
4. Stockpile topsoil stripped from the site separately from subsoil for reuse. Protect stockpiles from erosion with silt fence and temporary seeding if stockpiled for more than 14 days.

## Detail L-13: Seeding and Sodding Specifications

### Description

Seed mix and sod specifications for permanent turf establishment in Kentucky (USDA Zone 6b, transition zone).

### Permanent Seed Mixes (Kentucky)

**Mix A — General Purpose Lawn (Sun to Part Shade)**

| Species | Variety | Percentage by Weight | Rate (lbs/1,000 SF) |

| Kentucky Bluegrass | NTEP top-rated cultivar | 20% | 1-2 |
| **Total seeding rate** | | **100%** | **8-10** |

| Species | Variety | Percentage by Weight | Rate (lbs/1,000 SF) |

| **Total seeding rate** | | **100%** | **8-10** |

**Mix C — Native / Low-Maintenance (Detention Basins, Right-of-Way)**

| Species | Variety | Percentage by Weight | Rate (lbs/1,000 SF) |
|---------|---------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Tall Fescue (Turf-type or KY-31) | Certified | 70% | 5-6 |
| Annual Ryegrass (nurse crop) | Certified | 15% | 2 |
| White Clover | Certified | 15% | 1 |
| **Total seeding rate** | | **100%** | **8-9** |

### Seeding Calendar (Kentucky)

| Season | Dates | Preferred Mix | Notes |
|--------|-------|---------------|-------|
| Fall (primary) | September 1 - October 15 | All mixes | Best results; cool-season grasses establish rapidly |
| Spring (secondary) | March 1 - April 30 | Mix A, Mix B | Acceptable; crabgrass competition is a concern |
| Summer | May 1 - August 31 | Temporary cover only | Bermudagrass or annual ryegrass for erosion control |
| Winter (dormant) | November 15 - February 28 | Mix C only | Dormant seeding; germinates in spring |

### Sod Specifications

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Sod type | Turf-type tall fescue or tall fescue/Kentucky bluegrass blend |
| Sod thickness | 3/4-inch soil + roots (minimum); uniform thickness |
| Sod age | Harvested within 36 hours of delivery; protect from drying |
| Installation | Stagger joints (brick pattern); butt edges tight — no gaps or overlaps |
| Slopes | Peg with biodegradable sod staples on slopes steeper than 4:1 |
| Watering | Saturate sod and soil beneath immediately after installation; 1 inch water daily for first 2 weeks |
| Rolling | Roll with lightweight roller after installation to ensure root-soil contact |

### Notes

1. Fertilize all seeded and sodded areas per soil test recommendations. If no soil test, apply starter fertilizer (10-20-20) at 400 lbs/acre at the time of seeding/sodding.
2. Apply pelletized lime at the rate indicated by the soil test to achieve pH 6.0-6.5.
3. Provide temporary erosion control (ECB or straw mulch) on all seeded slopes until 80% turf coverage is achieved.
4. Apply straw mulch at 2 tons/acre (1 bale per 800 SF) on flat seeded areas. Crimp or tack with tackifier to prevent blowing.
5. Mow newly established turf when it reaches 4 inches in height. Do not remove more than 1/3 of the blade height per mowing.

## Detail L-14: Plant Spacing Tables

### Description

Standard spacing for common landscape plant types based on mature spread. Spacing is measured center-to-center.

### Shade Trees

| Mature Spread | Spacing (single row) | Spacing (staggered/double row) |
|---------------|---------------------|-------------------------------|
| 30-40 feet | 35-40 feet O.C. | 30-35 feet O.C. |
| 40-50 feet | 40-50 feet O.C. | 35-40 feet O.C. |
| 50+ feet | 50+ feet O.C. | 40-45 feet O.C. |

### Ornamental Trees

| Mature Spread | Spacing (single row) | Spacing (grouping) |
|---------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| 15-20 feet | 15-20 feet O.C. | 12-15 feet O.C. |
| 20-25 feet | 20-25 feet O.C. | 15-20 feet O.C. |

### Evergreen Trees (Screening)

| Mature Spread | Spacing (single row) | Spacing (staggered double row) |
|---------------|---------------------|-------------------------------|
| 8-12 feet | 8-10 feet O.C. | 6-8 feet O.C. (offset 1/2 spacing) |
| 12-20 feet | 10-15 feet O.C. | 8-12 feet O.C. |

### Shrubs

| Mature Spread | Spacing (mass planting) | Spacing (hedge) |
|---------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| 2-3 feet | 24-30 inches O.C. | 18-24 inches O.C. |
| 3-5 feet | 36-48 inches O.C. | 30-36 inches O.C. |
| 5-8 feet | 60-72 inches O.C. | 48-60 inches O.C. |
| 8-12 feet | 8-10 feet O.C. | 6-8 feet O.C. |

### Groundcovers

| Mature Spread | Spacing | Coverage Timeline |
|---------------|---------|-------------------|
| 6-12 inches | 8-12 inches O.C. (triangular pattern) | Full coverage in 1-2 seasons |
| 12-18 inches | 12-18 inches O.C. | Full coverage in 1-2 seasons |
| 18-24 inches | 18-24 inches O.C. | Full coverage in 2-3 seasons |

### Notes

1. Spacing shown is for landscape aesthetic purposes. Reduce spacing for faster screening; increase spacing for specimen display.
2. For mass plantings, use a triangular (offset) planting pattern for more uniform coverage compared to grid patterns.
3. Always verify mature size against site constraints: building setbacks, sight triangles, overhead utilities (25-foot radius from power lines for large trees), and underground utilities.
4. For street tree spacing, follow the local urban forestry standards (typically 30-40 feet O.C. for medium trees, 40-50 feet O.C. for large trees).

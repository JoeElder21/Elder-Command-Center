## Overview

The City of Elizabethtown is the county seat of Hardin County, Kentucky, located along the I-65 corridor approximately 45 miles south of Louisville. The city operates under a mayor-council form of government with planning and zoning administered by the Elizabethtown Planning & Zoning Commission. Zoning regulations are codified in the Elizabethtown Zoning Ordinance, adopted and amended by the City Council upon recommendation of the Planning Commission.

**Relevant JEDS Projects:**
- Staybridge Suites Elizabethtown (Critical) — Hospitality / Site Development
- Courtyard Marriott Elizabethtown (Critical) — Hospitality / Site Development

**Governing Body:** Elizabethtown Planning & Zoning Commission
**Appeals Body:** Elizabethtown Board of Adjustment
**Subdivision Authority:** Elizabethtown-Hardin County Planning Commission [VERIFY]

## Assumptions & Verification Notes

- All dimensional standards, parking ratios, and procedural requirements are based on typical Kentucky municipal zoning practices for cities of comparable size and are reasonable approximations. **Every value marked [VERIFY] must be confirmed with the current edition of the Elizabethtown Zoning Ordinance.**
- District designations (B-2, B-3, etc.) are based on common Kentucky zoning nomenclature. Elizabethtown may use different district labels. [VERIFY]
- Contact information has been intentionally omitted. Obtain current contacts from the City of Elizabethtown website or by calling the main city office. [VERIFY]
- Stormwater requirements may be governed at the county level (Hardin County) rather than city level. [VERIFY jurisdiction for stormwater review]
- KYTC encroachment permits are a separate process from local site plan review and run on independent timelines. Initiate KYTC applications early — they are typically the longest-lead regulatory item for highway-fronting commercial sites.
- Hotel brand standards (Marriott for Courtyard, IHG for Staybridge) impose additional design requirements beyond local zoning. Coordinate brand and zoning compliance concurrently to avoid design conflicts.
- Floodplain data must be verified with current FEMA FIRM panels. Preliminary or pending FIRM revisions (LOMRs, CLOMRs) may affect site constraints.

## Overview

Louisville/Jefferson County Metro Government (Louisville Metro) is a consolidated city-county government encompassing all of Jefferson County, Kentucky. It is the largest city in Kentucky by population. Land use regulation is governed by the Louisville Metro Land Development Code (LDC), which replaced the prior zoning ordinance upon merger in 2003. The LDC is administered by the Louisville Metro Department of Codes and Regulations (Planning and Design Services division). Louisville Metro uses a form district overlay system in addition to traditional zoning districts, which is a distinguishing feature of its regulatory framework.

The Metropolitan Sewer District (MSD) is a separate entity that administers stormwater management, sanitary sewer, and flood control throughout Jefferson County.

**Relevant JEDS Projects:**

**Governing Body:** Louisville Metro Council

## Form Districts

Louisville Metro's form district system is a critical regulatory layer. Form districts establish the physical form, pattern, and character of development — building placement, height, massing, and relationship to streets. Each property in Jefferson County is assigned to a form district, which operates as an overlay on the base zoning district.

### Form District Types

| Form District | Character | Typical Application | Notes |
|---------------|-----------|--------------------|----|
| Neighborhood | Traditional residential neighborhoods | Residential areas, local commercial | Low-scale, walkable |
| Traditional Marketplace Corridor (TMC) | Mixed-use commercial corridors | Arterial commercial strips | Moderate intensity |
| Traditional Neighborhood (TN) | Traditional urban grid neighborhoods | Older residential areas | Pedestrian-oriented |
| Traditional Workplace (TW) | Employment centers | Office/industrial parks | Auto-oriented but buffered |
| Suburban Marketplace Corridor (SMC) | Auto-oriented commercial | Suburban commercial strips | Higher intensity commercial |
| Suburban Workplace (SW) | Suburban employment | Office parks, light industrial | Larger setbacks |
| Village | Small-scale mixed-use node | Neighborhood centers | Pedestrian-scale |
| Town Center | Mixed-use activity center | Significant commercial nodes | Multi-story, mixed-use |
| Regional Center (RC) | Major commercial/employment | Malls, major retail, hospitals | Highest intensity |
| Campus (C) | Institutional campuses | Universities, medical centers | Master plan-driven |
| Downtown (DT) | Urban core | Downtown Louisville | Highest density, no setbacks |

## Assumptions & Verification Notes

- All dimensional standards, parking ratios, and procedural requirements are based on general knowledge of the Louisville Metro Land Development Code. **Every value marked [VERIFY] must be confirmed with the current edition of the LDC and MSD Design Manual.**
- The form district overlay is a critical regulatory layer unique to Louisville Metro. Failure to identify and apply the correct form district will result in non-compliant designs. Determine form district assignments for all project sites before beginning design.
- Louisville MSD is a separate entity from Louisville Metro government. MSD approval runs in parallel with PDS review but on its own timeline. Initiate MSD coordination early — MSD review is frequently the longest-lead item.
- Brewery wastewater is a significant regulatory issue. MSD industrial pretreatment requirements can add cost and complexity. Engage MSD Industrial Waste section during feasibility, not during construction documents.
- Louisville Metro's development review process is more complex and time-consuming than smaller Kentucky jurisdictions. Budget 3-6 months for development plan approval, longer for zone changes. [VERIFY current review timelines]
- Louisville Water Company (a separate public utility) provides water service. MSD provides sewer and stormwater. Both require coordination for capacity and connection approvals.
- Contact information has been intentionally omitted. Obtain current contacts from Louisville Metro and MSD websites. [VERIFY]
- The Cornerstone 2040 Comprehensive Plan guides land use decisions and is referenced in zone change applications. Verify that proposed uses are consistent with the comprehensive plan designation for each project site.

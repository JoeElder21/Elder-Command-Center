## Overview

The City of Bardstown is the county seat of Nelson County, Kentucky, located approximately 40 miles southeast of Louisville. Bardstown holds the distinction of being designated "The Bourbon Capital of the World" and is a significant tourism destination along the Kentucky Bourbon Trail. The city has an active historic preservation program with designated historic districts. Planning and zoning are administered by the Bardstown-Nelson County Planning Commission, which serves both the city and unincorporated areas of the county.

Bardstown's regulatory environment reflects its dual character: a growing community attracting commercial and residential development, and a historic town with preservation sensibilities. Projects within or adjacent to historic districts face additional review layers.

## Assumptions & Verification Notes

- All dimensional standards, parking ratios, and procedural requirements are based on typical Kentucky municipal zoning practices for cities of Bardstown's size. **Every value marked [VERIFY] must be confirmed with the current Bardstown-Nelson County Zoning Ordinance and Subdivision Regulations.**
- Bardstown's historic preservation regulations add a layer of review for projects within or adjacent to the historic district. Determine historic district boundaries early in the design process for all three projects.
- Nelson County unincorporated areas may have different zoning classifications and standards than within Bardstown city limits. Confirm which jurisdiction governs each project site. [VERIFY]
- Grand Lakes Phases 2-3 are dependent on Phase 1 infrastructure. Obtain Phase 1 as-built drawings and utility acceptance documentation before beginning Phase 2-3 design.
- Bardstown Commons mixed-use concept may require zoning that does not currently exist in the ordinance. If no MU district exists, a zone change to B-4 Planned Commercial or a PUD approach may be necessary. [VERIFY]
- St. Joseph Bardstown — religious properties have certain protections under the Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act (RLUIPA). If zoning poses a substantial burden on religious exercise, RLUIPA may provide additional legal grounds. Consult with legal counsel if zoning issues arise.
